# Resources for mod-112 — Career Positioning and the Next Step on the Ladder

Curated primary-source references for the module's chapters. Oriented around (a) the exec-communication craft the story inherits, (b) the deliberate-practice literature the development plan draws on, (c) the standards and credentialing bodies the external positioning signals through, (d) venues and publications the public-artefact set targets, and (e) the adjacent-role curricula this module routes leads toward.

Citations are to primary sources where available; where the surface shifts quickly (venue catalogues, exam dates), items are flagged for next-cycle re-verification.

## Exec-communication craft (Chapter 01)

- **Barbara Minto — *The Pyramid Principle: Logic in Writing and Thinking*** (Pearson Education, 3rd ed. 2009). The authoritative treatment of top-down communication (bottom-line-up-front / pyramid structure) that the four-move story draws on. Origin reading for Move 1.
- **Harvard Business Review — "The Executive's Guide to Better Listening" and related HBR classics on executive communication**. The HBR archive carries a running set of short pieces on exec writing craft usable as pattern libraries. Not a single citation; search HBR "executive communication" to the archive.
- **Carmine Gallo — *The Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs*** (McGraw-Hill, 2009) and ***Talk Like TED*** (St. Martin's Press, 2014). Pragmatic treatments of public-talk shape that extend the fifteen-minute-talk discipline from Chapter 01 into stage craft.
- **Chip Heath and Dan Heath — *Made to Stick: Why Some Ideas Survive and Others Die*** (Random House, 2007). The SUCCES frame (simple, unexpected, concrete, credible, emotional, stories) is a useful lens on the three-claim selection in Move 2.

## Deliberate-practice literature (Chapter 03)

- **K. Anders Ericsson, Ralf Th. Krampe, and Clemens Tesch-Römer — "The Role of Deliberate Practice in the Acquisition of Expert Performance"**, *Psychological Review*, 100(3), 363–406 (1993). Foundational paper; the "deliberate practice" term in this chapter's title is from here.
- **K. Anders Ericsson and Robert Pool — *Peak: Secrets from the New Science of Expertise*** (Houghton Mifflin Harcourt, 2016). The accessible synthesis of the deliberate-practice research for the practitioner audience.
- **Carol S. Dweck — *Mindset: The New Psychology of Success*** (Random House, 2006, updated edition 2016). The growth-mindset literature complements deliberate practice; both are prerequisites to the energised-vs.-skilled distinction Chapter 02 makes.

## Credentials and standards signalled externally (Chapter 04)

- **IAPP AIGP (AI Governance Professional) — body of knowledge and exam.** [iapp.org/certify/aigp](https://iapp.org/certify/aigp/). Introduced 2024. The AI governance credential most read externally for paths A and B. IAPP's continuing-education calendar is the renewal surface.
- **ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Management system.** [iso.org/standard/81230.html](https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html). The AI management system standard; the Lead Implementer and Lead Auditor credentials are issued by accredited training providers against this standard. The 2023 first edition remains the current global edition; the EN ISO/IEC 42001:2026 CEN adoption carries no technical change to the standard's requirements. ISO reviews management-system standards on a rolling cycle, so the lead confirms the standard's status before leaning on edition specifics.
- **NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) 1.0 and Generative AI Profile.** [nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework). The US voluntary framework the AIGP body of knowledge and the enablement-side policy stack both reference.
- **EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).** [eur-lex.europa.eu](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:L_202401689) / [artificialintelligenceact.eu](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/). The foundational regulation Article 4 draws from; required reading for AIGP vocabulary.
- **Prosci — Change Management Certification and ADKAR model.** [prosci.com](https://www.prosci.com/). The flagship change-management credential; ADKAR is the diagnostic model the credential teaches.
- **ATD — APTD and CPTD credentials, Talent Development Capability Model.** [td.org/certification](https://www.td.org/certification). The talent-development credentials that the partner training-curriculum lead typically holds.
- **PMI — Project Management Professional (PMP).** [pmi.org/certifications/project-management-pmp](https://www.pmi.org/certifications/project-management-pmp). The program-management lingua franca at enterprise scale.
- **IAPP — related AI / privacy credentials (AIGP, CIPP/E, CIPP/US, CIPM, CIPT).** [iapp.org/certify](https://iapp.org/certify/). Useful adjacency reading when the lead's partner surface includes privacy counsel.

Renewal cadences, continuing-education requirements, and current-cycle exam fees shift annually across all of the above bodies; the lead confirms the specific numbers on each credential body's site before committing to a cycle.

## Conference-talk venues by path (Chapter 05)

### Governance-side (paths A and B)

- **IAPP Global Privacy Summit and IAPP AI Governance Global** — [iapp.org/conference](https://iapp.org/conference/). The primary IAPP convenings; AI Governance Global is the AIGP audience's event.
- **ACM FAccT (Fairness, Accountability, and Transparency)** — [facctconference.org](https://facctconference.org/). The academic-industry conference on AI fairness and governance.
- **OECD Global Forum on Technology** — [oecd.ai](https://oecd.ai/). Policy-adjacent convening; useful for leads positioning toward public-facing governance work.

### Executive-side (path C)

- **The AI Conference** ([aiconference.com](https://aiconference.com/)) and **World Summit AI** ([worldsummit.ai](https://worldsummit.ai/)) — representative enterprise-AI practitioner convenings. The landscape of these events still reshapes year to year; the lead confirms the current cycle's shape before committing to a submission.
- **Stanford HAI corporate programme events and MIT CSAIL industry events** — [hai.stanford.edu](https://hai.stanford.edu/); [csail.mit.edu](https://www.csail.mit.edu/). Invited-panel surfaces used by executive-leaning practitioners.
- **NACD (National Association of Corporate Directors) AI events** — [nacdonline.org](https://www.nacdonline.org/). Board-level convenings for the CAIO-adjacent audience.

### Engineering-side (path D)

- **LeadDev** — [leaddev.com](https://leaddev.com/). The engineering-manager and engineering-leadership community; the Live and New York / London events.
- **QCon** — [qconferences.com](https://qconferences.com/). Software-engineering practitioner conference with AI tracks.
- **AI Engineer World's Fair / AI Engineer Summit** — [ai.engineer](https://ai.engineer/). The AI-engineering practitioner convening.
