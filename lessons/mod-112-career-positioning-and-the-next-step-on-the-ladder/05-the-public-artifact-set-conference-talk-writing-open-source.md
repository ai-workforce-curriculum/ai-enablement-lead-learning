# The Public Artifact Set — Conference Talk, Writing, Open-Source Enablement Artefact

> The signature artefacts from [Chapter 03](03-the-deliberate-development-plan-toward-the-chosen-next-step.md) establish depth in the lead's current organisation; the credentials positioning in [Chapter 04](04-positioning-the-credentials-portfolio-externally.md) signals the shared vocabulary of the chosen path. Neither reaches an audience the lead has not already met. The public artifact set does — three artefacts, each in a different medium, each visible to a stranger who has never heard of the lead. The chapter authors the set at module altitude: the three artefacts, the standard each is held to, and the discipline of shipping them inside the preparation window.

## Why three, and why these three

A single public artefact can be lucky or borrowed. A one-off conference talk can be a slot a mentor arranged; a single viral LinkedIn post can be a moment rather than a position. Three artefacts across three mediums, shipped within an 18-to-24 month window, defeats the "lucky once" read. The combination is what signals a practitioner with a position, not a lead who happened to get on a stage.

The three mediums each do a different job:

- **The conference talk** — spoken, presence-based, a one-hour authored slot in front of an audience that chose to be there. The talk is the live-signal artefact. Attendees remember a talk differently than they remember an article; the talk is where the lead's story in [Chapter 01](01-the-exec-communication-story-that-ties-the-track-together.md) becomes the lead's professional voice.
- **The piece of writing** — asynchronous, searchable, re-readable, quotable. The writing is the authored-record artefact. A reader can find the piece months after publication, cite it, forward it, come to it from search results. Writing compounds in a way talks do not.
- **The open-source enablement artefact** — a codebase, a template bundle, a measurement framework, a prompt library at operating depth. The open-source artefact is the leverage artefact. An artefact a dozen other organisations use is an artefact whose author is known by a hundred people who have never met the lead.

Together, the three carry a triangulated signal. Someone who has seen the talk can then read the writing and recognise the voice. Someone who has read the writing can then use the open-source artefact and recognise the operating discipline. Someone who has used the open-source artefact can then attend the talk and recognise the posture. The triangulation is what establishes the L45-and-above tier credibility the module spec is after.

## The standard each artefact is held to

The three are not three of the same thing. Each has a specific standard.

### The conference talk — specific talk, specific venue, specific audience

**What it is.** A 20-to-60-minute authored presentation at a conference the chosen path's audience attends. Not a webinar the lead organised; not a lunch-and-learn re-recorded. A programme-committee-accepted slot at a venue the audience reads as external and curated.

**The venue choice depends on the path.**

- **Path A / B (governance-side):** IAPP's Global Privacy Summit or Privacy. Security. Risk. + AI Governance Global, FAccT (ACM Fairness, Accountability, and Transparency), The AI Standards Hub Global Summit, convenings run by NIST's AI consortium (the body renamed from the AI Safety Institute Consortium in 2026), the OECD Global Forum on Technology. Industry-specific governance conferences for the lead's industry (HIMSS for health, Money 20/20 for finance). Specific venue names and dates shift year to year; the lead confirms the current cycle's shape before committing to a submission window.
- **Path C (CAIO):** Enterprise AI conferences (The AI Conference, World Summit AI, and similar practitioner-and-executive convenings that reshape year to year), executive education invited panels (Stanford HAI corporate programme events, MIT CSAIL industry events), board-level AI programmes (NACD's Directors Summit and Leading Minds of Tech cadence).
- **Path D (engineering manager):** LeadDev, QCon, Strange Loop (archive), re:Invent / AWS Summit AI sessions, Google Cloud Next AI tracks, AI Engineer summit. The engineering-adjacent conferences the applied-ai-engineer peer surface attends.

**The talk's shape.** The four-move story from [Chapter 01](01-the-exec-communication-story-that-ties-the-track-together.md) at fifteen-to-forty-five minutes. Opens with the organisational problem statement, walks the three evidence-backed claims, names the misses without theatre, closes with the ask — reframed for a conference audience as "what the audience should take away and act on." Not a program tour; a focused talk on one narrative.

**The acceptance bar.** Conference programme committees accept talks on three criteria: the speaker's credibility (which the credentials, the written artefact, or the open-source artefact can carry), the specificity of the claim (one specific operating problem, not "AI adoption"), and the evidence density of the proposal (what will the audience actually see, in detail, that they could not see in a vendor talk). A proposal that fails any of the three is declined; a proposal that clears all three is accepted on first submission.

**Common reasons the talk slips the preparation window.**

- The lead proposes to speak before they have the evidence. A talk on "scaling the enablement operating model" without a specific operating record that stretches at least two years will not pass programme review.
- The lead chooses a venue where the audience is not the next-step audience. A governance talk delivered to a product-manager audience does not reach the hiring committee for `head-of-ai-governance`.
- The lead treats the talk as a single effort rather than a programme. A rejected proposal is re-pitched, reworked, re-submitted to a sibling venue; a programme-accepted slot one year in is a reasonable cadence.

### The open piece of writing — specific publication, specific argument, specific length

**What it is.** A published long-form piece at a venue whose readership includes the chosen path's audience. Not a LinkedIn post; not an internal memo re-published. A piece whose venue, length, and argument the lead chose deliberately.

**The publication choice depends on the path.**

- **Path A / B (governance-side):** IAPP's The Privacy Advisor, the Lawfare AI series, practitioner commentary tied to the NIST AI RMF, industry-specific journals (e.g., ABA Business Law Today for finance / legal; JAMA perspectives for health governance), and edited standards-body publications (IEEE Spectrum AI governance pieces, ISO's standards-adoption case studies). Several of these publications move between invited-only and open submission across a cycle; the lead confirms the current submission path before pitching.
- **Path C (CAIO):** Harvard Business Review, MIT Sloan Management Review, Deloitte Insights, McKinsey Quarterly, The Information, Stratechery guest pieces. The board-and-executive audience reads a specific set of publications; a well-placed piece there is read by the board members the lead is positioning toward.
- **Path D (engineering manager):** LeadDev articles, the StaffEng site, ACM Queue, IEEE Software, Thoughtworks Technology Radar essays, the lead's own technical-blog publication at engineering-manager altitude. Engineering-manager audiences read for a different voice than governance or exec audiences — practical, systems-level, often code-adjacent.

**The writing's shape.** Approximately 2,000–4,000 words. Argues one specific claim with specific evidence. Not a survey; not a "ten things I learned" post. A single-thesis piece that a reader will quote by one sentence after reading.

**Three common writing shapes that land:**

- **The disciplined case study.** One organisational problem, the enablement response, the evidence, the honest read on what did not work. Approximately 3,000 words. Reads as authored practice.
- **The reading-the-literature piece.** One body of literature (the AI productivity empirical corpus, the AI governance standards, the change-management literature on technology adoption), a specific read of it, a specific application to the lead's operating context. Approximately 2,500 words. Reads as thoughtful positioning.
- **The pattern-and-anti-pattern piece.** A specific operating pattern, the anti-pattern it guards against, worked examples of each. Approximately 2,000 words. Reads as practitioner synthesis.

**The acceptance bar.** Edited publications read for the same three criteria as conferences — credibility, specificity, evidence density — but with a fourth: writing quality. The piece will be edited; the lead has to be willing to work with an editor and to produce three to five drafts.

**Common reasons the writing slips.**

- The lead writes the piece without a specific publication target, then shops it after the draft is done. Editorial alignment comes from targeting the publication first; retro-fitting a piece to a venue is low-yield.
- The lead writes too long. 6,000 words is a manuscript, not a piece; most venues will not accept it and most readers will not finish it. Hold to 2,000–4,000.
- The lead treats the piece as self-promotion rather than argument. The piece lands when the argument is the main character and the lead is the author; the piece does not land when the lead is the main character.

### The open-source enablement artefact — specific repository, specific scope, specific maintenance

**What it is.** A published, versioned, usable open-source artefact — a repository, a package, a template bundle, a data model — that someone outside the lead's organisation can pick up and use. Not a one-off gist; not an internal tool re-published without maintenance. An artefact whose README, examples, contribution guide, and licence are complete.

**Specific shapes the artefact can take.**

- **A prompt library at operating depth** — role-based prompt sets organised against the audience segmentation from [mod-104](../mod-104-training-programs-and-ai-literacy-curriculum/README.md), with evaluation criteria and versioning discipline. Shipped at a depth that the reader can use the library against a real workflow, not a generic "awesome-prompts" collection.
- **A playbook template bundle** — the rollout artefact bundle from [mod-102](../mod-102-selecting-and-rolling-out-ai-tools/README.md) (use policy, data-handling SOP, access and offboarding playbook, training package shell, support and escalation path, champion list, kill-switch and rollback plan) rendered as a fillable repository template. Templates for the use-policy variants (opt-in, mandate), templates for the kill-switch wording, templates for the champion-recruitment brief.
- **A measurement framework** — the three-cadence measurement bundle from [mod-106](../mod-106-outcome-measurement-and-executive-reporting/README.md) rendered as a running code artefact. Scorecard templates in a specific analytics stack, the misses-first discipline encoded as a review checklist, the ROI confidence-band calculation as runnable code.
- **A CoE operating toolkit** — the service catalog, intake form, Monday-triage routing rules, decision-log template, weekly status report template, risk register, vendor portfolio schema from [mod-111](../mod-111-ai-coe-operations-vendor-management-and-cross-functional-leadership/README.md) rendered as a versioned repository. One of the highest-leverage artefacts because the whole operating loop can be bootstrapped from it.

**The artefact's shape.** A README that explains what the artefact is, who it is for, how to use it, and what the artefact deliberately does not do. A LICENSE (Apache 2.0, MIT, or similar permissive licence; sometimes Creative Commons for documentation-only artefacts). A CONTRIBUTING file. At least one worked example. A versioning cadence. Two to five external users within a year of publication.

**Standard of maintenance.** The artefact is not a one-shot publication; it is a maintained artefact. The lead triages issues, merges reasonable PRs, bumps versions on a predictable cadence, and maintains a short changelog. An open-source artefact that was pushed and then abandoned reads worse than no artefact at all — it signals to the next-step audience that the lead ships but does not maintain.

**Common reasons the artefact slips.**

- The lead publishes a repository that is internal tooling with the names changed. External users cannot adopt it because it carries internal assumptions. The correction is to design the artefact from the start as external-reader-first, not as internal-with-redactions.
- The lead publishes and abandons. The repository has four stale PRs and three unanswered issues a year in. The signal reverses. A lead who cannot commit to the maintenance should ship a smaller, finished artefact rather than a bigger, abandoned one.
- The lead chooses a scope they cannot own. A full "enterprise AI enablement platform" open-source project is a multi-year, multi-contributor effort; it is not an individual signalling artefact. A tightly scoped template bundle or framework is.

## The sequencing within the preparation window

The three artefacts cannot all ship in the same quarter. Each takes months; each builds on the others. The common sequencing pattern:

- **Months 1–6:** draft the open-source artefact and the first piece of writing in parallel. The open-source artefact is drafted but not yet published; the writing is drafted and sent to an editor.
- **Months 4–9:** publish the open-source artefact, iterate the writing with the editor to publication. The open-source artefact is picked up by a few external users; the writing lands at the chosen publication.
- **Months 8–14:** propose the conference talk. The proposal cites the open-source artefact and the writing as evidence of practice; the programme committee has material to evaluate. The acceptance lands in month 10–12 for a conference 4–6 months out.
- **Months 12–18:** deliver the talk. Archive the slides and recording; cite them in the subsequent LinkedIn and CV updates.
- **Months 18–24:** refresh — second writing piece, open-source artefact v2, second talk (often at a sibling venue the first talk opened).

The sequencing matters because the three artefacts reinforce each other. A conference talk proposal that cites a published open-source artefact and a published piece of writing is a strong proposal. A conference talk proposal with no supporting artefacts is weaker; a proposal with only unpublished promises is weakest. The order builds the support structure for the talk, which is the hardest of the three to land cold.

## When one of the three cannot ship

Sometimes a lead cannot ship all three in the window. Travel constraints make the conference impractical; organisational policy blocks the open-source artefact; a major incident absorbs the quarter the writing was supposed to land in. The response is not to fake the artefact; it is to adapt the set:

- **Conference talk blocked by travel or caregiving constraints.** Substitute an invited panel at a virtual conference, a keynote at a vendor user conference (less prestige, still external), or a podcast interview series on the lead's thesis — spoken signal in a different medium.
- **Open-source artefact blocked by organisational policy.** Negotiate specifically for a redacted-and-templated version; most organisations will approve a de-identified template bundle even if they will not approve an internal tool's source. If that fails, substitute a published case study or a public tool review that carries the artefact's thesis without the code.
- **Writing blocked by a quarter.** Defer. Writing is the most time-shiftable of the three; a piece deferred by a quarter is a piece that lands in the preparation window.

The adaptation preserves the three-medium triangulation. The adaptation that collapses to a single medium — three conference talks, no writing, no open-source — produces a one-dimensional signal; the audience reads "talker" rather than "practitioner."

## Two failure modes to pre-empt

### Failure mode 1 — the artefact set is impressive but not aligned to the path

The lead ships a well-received conference talk, a published piece, and an open-source artefact — and the chosen path's hiring committee reads them and asks "who is this for?" The talk was on prompt engineering for non-engineers; the piece was on AI-in-L&D measurement; the open-source artefact was a champion-network charter template. Each landed well in its own audience. None spoke to the governance committee hiring for `head-of-ai-governance`.

The correction is to run the artefact set against the Chapter 02 path decision explicitly, before the artefact is drafted. If the three artefacts would not read as evidence-of-position to the chosen path's audience, the artefact is misaligned — no matter how well it is executed.

### Failure mode 2 — the artefact set is aligned but the operating evidence does not back it

The lead ships a talk on "running the enablement operating loop at scale" with the artefact set behind it — and when the next-step hiring committee pulls the lead into the interview round, the operating-evidence record ([Chapter 03](03-the-deliberate-development-plan-toward-the-chosen-next-step.md)'s signature artefacts) does not match the public claim. The talk promised two years of operating depth; the signature artefacts show six months of it with gaps.

The correction is to lead with operating evidence and follow with public artefacts, not the reverse. The Chapter 03 signature artefacts are shipped first; the public artefacts narrate what the signature artefacts have proved. A public artefact set that runs ahead of the operating evidence is a positioning overreach, and the hiring conversation catches it.

## Summary

- **The public artifact set is three artefacts in three mediums** — one conference talk, one piece of writing, one open-source enablement artefact — shipped within an 18-to-24 month window. Three establishes a position; one is a moment.
- **Each medium does a different job:** the talk is live signal, the writing is authored record, the open-source artefact is leverage. Together they triangulate the lead's position to an audience that has not met the lead.
- **The venue, publication, and repository shape depend on the chosen path from [Chapter 02](02-choosing-the-next-step-on-the-ladder.md).** A governance talk at an engineering conference misses the audience the lead needs; the path discipline governs the venue choice.
- **The three artefacts sequence** — draft writing and open-source in parallel in months 1–6, publish both by months 8–9, propose and deliver the talk by months 12–18. The earlier two artefacts are the support structure for the talk proposal.
- **Adapt the set rather than fake it when a constraint blocks one medium.** A panel can substitute for a keynote; a redacted template can substitute for an internal tool; a quarter-deferred piece is still a piece. Do not collapse to one medium.
- **Two failure modes to pre-empt:** the set that is well-executed but misaligned to the path, and the set that outruns the operating evidence behind it. The Chapter 02 decision and the Chapter 03 signature artefacts are the twin discipline that prevents both.
