# Exercise 04 — Public Artifact Set Plan

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours
**Format:** three-artefact plan with sequencing calendar, Markdown
**Grounding chapters:** [05 — The Public Artifact Set: Conference Talk, Writing, Open-Source Enablement Artefact](../05-the-public-artifact-set-conference-talk-writing-open-source.md); [03 — The Deliberate Development Plan Toward the Chosen Next Step](../03-the-deliberate-development-plan-toward-the-chosen-next-step.md) for the signature-artefact alignment. Supporting reads: [04 — Positioning the Credentials Portfolio Externally](../04-positioning-the-credentials-portfolio-externally.md).

## Objective

Author the specific plan for the three public artefacts — the conference talk, the piece of writing, the open-source enablement artefact — that will ship across the 18-to-24-month preparation window. The plan has to be specific enough to execute against (named venues, named publications, named repository scope) and sequenced enough to produce the compounding effect Chapter 05 specifies.

The plan is a working document, not a wishlist. Each artefact has a named target, a named milestone, and a named owner (usually the lead; sometimes a collaborator). An artefact plan the lead cannot imagine executing is a plan to re-write; name the artefacts you can actually ship.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter 05 of this module read; Chapter 03 for the signature-artefact alignment; Chapter 04 for the credentials positioning that interacts with the bio.
- The path chosen in [Exercise 02](exercise-02-next-step-ladder-decision-worked-example.md).
- The signature-artefact set from [Exercise 02](exercise-02-next-step-ladder-decision-worked-example.md)'s decision memo, either explicitly named or inferable from the chosen path. The public artefacts are the outward-facing narration of what the signature artefacts have proved; Chapter 05's failure mode 2 catches the misalignment.
- An honest read on your writing rate and travel capacity. A plan that names three artefacts a lead cannot ship in the window is a plan that produces guilt rather than preparation.

## Problem statement

The lead has chosen a path and the preparation window is open. The CV and LinkedIn renderings from [Exercise 03](exercise-03-credentials-portfolio-external-positioning.md) have blank slots where the public artefacts would appear ("Featured talk — TBD," "Author of — TBD," "Maintainer of — TBD"). The gap is specific. The exercise closes it with a specific plan.

## Requirements

### Part A — the artefact alignment check

Approximately 150–200 words.

Walk the three artefacts against the chosen path and against the signature-artefact set from Exercise 02. For each of the three mediums (talk, writing, open-source), name:

- The one-sentence argument the artefact carries — the thesis an audience should take away after encountering it.
- The specific signature artefact (from the Exercise 02 plan) that the public artefact narrates.
- The path-audience the public artefact is calibrated for.

If any of the three mediums does not carry a thesis aligned with the chosen path, name the misalignment and the correction. Chapter 05's failure mode 1 (impressive but misaligned set) is the specific drift this part guards against.

### Part B — the conference talk plan

Approximately 200–300 words.

Produce:

1. **The talk title and subtitle.** Not "AI in the enterprise"; specific. "Running the enablement-side incident path: the first forty-five minutes from signal to governance handoff" is the shape.
2. **The talk abstract** — the programme-committee submission version, approximately 150–200 words. States the organisational problem, the three evidence surfaces the talk walks, the audience takeaway. The abstract is written for the committee, not for the audience — the committee reads for credibility, specificity, and evidence density.
3. **The venue short list** — three to five target conferences, in priority order. For each, name the submission window and the typical acceptance cadence. If a venue's details are not reliably known, mark the entry `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` and move to the next.
4. **The prerequisite support.** What the lead will cite in the proposal — the open-source artefact and the writing piece from Parts C and D, specific experience bullets, any prior speaking record. The support structure is what carries a cold proposal past the first screening round.
5. **The specific milestone cadence.** Submission date for the first venue, decision expected date, fallback plan if declined (next venue in the list), target delivery date.

### Part C — the writing piece plan

Approximately 200–300 words.

Produce:

1. **The piece's one-line argument.** The single sentence a reader will quote after reading. Write it first; everything else follows from it.
2. **The piece shape.** Chapter 05 names three: the disciplined case study, the reading-the-literature piece, the pattern-and-anti-pattern piece. Name the chosen shape and why it fits the argument.
3. **The publication target short list** — two to three publications in priority order, matched to the path. For each, name the submission or pitch process (editor contact, open submission, invited-only). If a publication requires an existing relationship, name the relationship or the pitch-cold path.
4. **The outline** — five to seven bullet points carrying the argument from the one-line thesis through the specific evidence to the closing synthesis. The outline is what the editor will read first; invest in it.
5. **The specific milestone cadence.** Outline complete date, first draft date, editor contact date, publication target date. Writing slips more than talks do; the cadence has to carry a buffer.

### Part D — the open-source artefact plan

Approximately 200–300 words.

Produce:

1. **The artefact's name and one-line description.** What it is and who it is for.
2. **The specific scope** — the four or five components the v0.1 repository will contain (README, LICENSE, CONTRIBUTING, at least one worked example, versioning cadence, a deliberately-not-included list). The "deliberately-not-included" list is as important as the included list — an artefact that tries to cover everything cannot ship.
3. **The maintenance commitment.** How often issues will be triaged (weekly / fortnightly / monthly), the PR-merge cadence, the versioning cadence, the deprecation policy. Maintain is the hard part; Chapter 05 names the "published and abandoned" failure mode as the most common.
4. **The adoption target** — how many external users (people / organisations) the lead is targeting within six months and twelve months of publication. Realistic numbers. "One thousand stars" is a wrong target for a template bundle; "two to five organisations using the templates in their own operating setup" is the right shape.
5. **The specific milestone cadence.** Repository scaffold date, v0.1 publication date, first external adoption target date, v0.2 release date.

### Part E — the sequencing calendar

A single-page calendar (text or table format is fine) laying out the 18-to-24-month sequencing from Chapter 05. Rows are months (M1 through M24); columns are the three artefacts; cells name the specific milestone landing in that month.

Specifically mark:

- The months each artefact is in active work.
- The months each artefact is parked (waiting on an editor, a programme committee, an external user signal).
- The two or three months where two artefacts are in active work simultaneously — the lead's capacity has to clear these.
- The month the three artefacts are "in the world" together, with the lead's external surfaces updated to reflect the complete set.

### Part F — the adaptation contingencies

Three short paragraphs (approximately 50–75 words each) on Chapter 05's adaptation guidance:

- The adaptation if the conference talk is blocked by travel or scheduling constraints.
- The adaptation if the open-source artefact is blocked by organisational policy.
- The adaptation if the writing piece slips by a quarter.

Each paragraph names the specific adaptation the lead would make. The adaptations are contingency plans; they are not necessary at the plan date but have to be pre-thought so a late constraint does not collapse the artefact set to a single medium.

## Starter guidance

- Write the one-line argument for the writing piece first. Everything in Parts B, C, D should be reconcilable to the one-line argument of the writing piece — it is the simplest, most distilled statement of the lead's thesis; if the talk and the open-source cannot be derived from it, the set is probably not triangulated.
- Pick the writing publication before drafting the outline. Editorial alignment comes from targeting the publication first. An outline written without a publication target is an outline that will be re-worked when the publication is chosen; the re-work is pure overhead.
- The open-source artefact scope is almost always too large on first draft. The "deliberately-not-included" list is where the cut is made. If the v0.1 repository has more than six components in scope, re-read the scope.
- Check Part E for the "two artefacts in active work simultaneously" months. More than two of those months in the window is a plan the lead will not execute; rebalance by shifting one artefact's milestones later. The sequencing exists because concurrent public-artefact production burns the lead out.
- Where a venue's submission dates, a publication's editor contact, or a repository's naming convention is not reliably known, mark `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` and continue. The research for these open items is first-quarter preparation work, not Exercise 04 work.
- Do not include numbers you cannot commit to. "500 stars on GitHub" is a wrong adoption target if the lead does not yet have the audience to drive it. "Two to five organisations adopt the templates" is realistic for a well-scoped template bundle; use that frame.

## Deliverables

- `alignment-check.md` — the Part A artefact-alignment walk.
- `conference-talk-plan.md` — the Part B plan including title, abstract, venue list, support, milestones.
- `writing-plan.md` — the Part C plan including argument, shape, publication list, outline, milestones.
- `open-source-plan.md` — the Part D plan including name, scope, maintenance commitment, adoption target, milestones.
- `sequencing-calendar.md` — the Part E 18-to-24-month calendar.
- `adaptation-contingencies.md` — the Part F three adaptations.
- A short cover note (3–5 sentences) stating the one-line argument shared across the three artefacts, the artefact the lead is most and least confident about shipping, and one dependency the plan has on a specific partner (sponsor, editor, co-maintainer).

## Acceptance criteria

- Part A names a one-sentence thesis for each of the three artefacts and maps each to a specific signature artefact from Exercise 02's plan.
- Part A explicitly confirms (or diagnoses) the alignment between the three artefacts' theses and the chosen path.
- Part B's abstract is approximately 150–200 words and written for a programme committee's criteria (credibility, specificity, evidence density).
- Part B's venue list is in priority order with a submission window noted for each.
- Part C's one-line argument is literally one sentence — not a paragraph.
- Part C's outline is five to seven bullets that could carry an editor's first read of the piece's structure.
- Part D's scope has a "deliberately-not-included" list.
- Part D's maintenance commitment names a specific triage / merge / version cadence.
- Part D's adoption target is realistic (not a star-count aspiration; a specific user / organisation shape).
- Part E's calendar carries all three artefacts' milestones across M1–M24 and marks the simultaneous-work months.
- Part E has at most two months of "two artefacts in active work simultaneously"; if more, the plan is rebalanced.
- Part F covers all three adaptations with specific alternative moves.
- `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` appears wherever a venue / publication / repository detail is uncertain. No guessed submission dates.
- The cover note names the one-line argument, the most and least confident artefact, and one partner dependency.

## Stretch goals

- **Produce the proposal draft.** The conference-talk submission email or form, filled out as if it were being sent this week. The act of writing the submission draft forces the plan to confront its evidence base.
- **Author the editor-outreach note.** A two-paragraph pitch to the writing piece's priority publication, including the one-line argument, the outline, and the lead's credential context. The note is not sent at exercise time; it is drafted so it can be sent on the Part C milestone date.
- **Produce a v0.1 repository scaffold.** Create the actual repository (public or private; publicly released at Part D's v0.1 milestone date). Include the README, LICENSE, CONTRIBUTING, and one placeholder worked example. The scaffold is the first commitment that forces the plan past aspiration.
- **Map the three artefacts back onto the Exercise 03 LinkedIn Featured section curation plan.** The Featured section's rolling plan has three slots; name which artefact fills which slot at which milestone. This closes the loop between external positioning and public-artefact production.

## Why this exercise matters

Many leads write a one-sentence next-year goal like "give a talk this year" and never progress. The reason is that the goal has no specific venue, no specific argument, no supporting artefacts, no sequencing. This exercise is the discipline of converting the goal into a plan the lead could hand to a careful stranger and expect them to execute. The plan is why some leads reach the hiring conversation in year two with three artefacts in hand, while others reach it in year four with none.
