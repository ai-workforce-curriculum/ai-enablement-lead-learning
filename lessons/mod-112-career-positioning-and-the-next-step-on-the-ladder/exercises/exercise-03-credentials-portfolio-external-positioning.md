# Exercise 03 — Credentials Portfolio External Positioning

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours
**Format:** three external-surface renderings (CV extract, LinkedIn profile extract, public bio), Markdown
**Grounding chapters:** [04 — Positioning the Credentials Portfolio Externally](../04-positioning-the-credentials-portfolio-externally.md). Prior reading: [mod-111 Chapter 07](../../mod-111-ai-coe-operations-vendor-management-and-cross-functional-leadership/07-the-credentials-portfolio-as-shared-vocabulary.md) (the portfolio at internal altitude); [mod-101 Chapter 06](../../mod-101-role-and-the-org-wide-enablement-operating-model/06-credentials-and-the-90-day-entry-plan.md) (the credentials primer).

## Objective

Render the credentials portfolio across the three external surfaces from Chapter 04 — the CV, the LinkedIn profile, and the public-facing bio — calibrated to the path chosen in [Exercise 02](exercise-02-next-step-ladder-decision-worked-example.md). The output is a working update kit the lead can apply to their external surfaces in the week after the exercise.

The exercise is honesty-constrained in a specific way. Do not render a credential you do not hold. For the chosen lead record, use the credentials actually held; if the record is a worked-example lead, specify which credentials the worked-example lead holds before you start Part A. Padding the portfolio to improve the rendering is a failure of the exercise's purpose, not a stretch.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter 04 of this module read.
- [mod-111 Chapter 07](../../mod-111-ai-coe-operations-vendor-management-and-cross-functional-leadership/07-the-credentials-portfolio-as-shared-vocabulary.md) read; the internal portfolio should already be named for the lead record.
- A chosen path from [Exercise 02](exercise-02-next-step-ladder-decision-worked-example.md). The exercise is path-dependent; you cannot render the subset without the path.
- A current snapshot of the portfolio — which credentials are held at exam level, which are held at body-of-knowledge-conversational level, which are in progress (studying but not yet certified), which are lapsed (held historically but not renewed).

## Problem statement

The lead's existing external surfaces (CV, LinkedIn, speaker bio) were last updated before the path decision was made. The credentials section is either an unselected list of everything held (reads as accumulation) or missing (reads as absence). The lead has an incoming surface update to do — refreshing the LinkedIn after a recent speaking engagement, renewing the CV ahead of an internal mobility conversation, submitting a bio to a conference programme committee. The surfaces will be read in the next thirty days.

This exercise produces the specific renderings for each surface, path-calibrated, ready to apply.

## Requirements

### Part A — the portfolio snapshot

A one-page honest inventory of the lead's current portfolio. Not for external use; the authoring prerequisite for Parts B–D.

Columns: credential name, issuing body, year obtained (or year studied / in progress), current status (held and current / held and lapsed / in progress / planned for next year / held only at conversational depth and not certified), where the credential is used in the operating ritual today.

Include at minimum the five positions from [mod-111 Chapter 07](../../mod-111-ai-coe-operations-vendor-management-and-cross-functional-leadership/07-the-credentials-portfolio-as-shared-vocabulary.md): AIGP, ATD CPTD, Prosci Change Management, ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Implementer / Lead Auditor, PMP. Add any additional credentials the lead actually holds (executive-education programs, cloud certifications, industry-specific credentials like CIPP / CIPT / CIPM, change-management alternatives like CCMP, standards-body memberships).

If the lead-record is you and your snapshot is small — one or two credentials held — that is the honest starting point. The renderings in Parts B–D will reflect that starting point; the preparation plan ([Exercise 04](exercise-04-public-artifact-set-plan.md)) names what to add inside the window.

### Part B — the CV rendering

The CV's credential-adjacent sections, rendered for the chosen path. Produce:

1. **The role summary paragraph** — three to four sentences at the top of the CV, naming the lead's position, the operating scope (number of BUs, program budget range if public, program scope), and one or two credentials used as positioning modifiers. The paragraph should land the chosen path's primary signal without naming the path itself.
2. **The "Credentials & Standards Vocabulary" section** — a named section, placed below the role summary and above experience bullets. Three to five credentials named, each with one line of context in the "held at [altitude], operating use in [specific surface]" shape. Not a list; a specifically-authored subset.
3. **Two experience bullets that cite credentials in use** — two of the lead's experience bullets rewritten to show the credential's operating depth, not just its possession. "Authored and shipped the enablement-side policy stack against ISO/IEC 42001 Annex A controls; led the quarterly governance-council reading of the policy-to-control mapping." The bullet carries the credential's signal through the work.

Approximately 250–350 words total.

### Part C — the LinkedIn rendering

The LinkedIn-profile credential-adjacent sections, rendered for the chosen path. Produce:

1. **The About section first paragraph** — approximately 60–100 words. Opens with the path-signalling framing from [Chapter 04](../04-positioning-the-credentials-portfolio-externally.md) ("AI governance practitioner with operational depth in…" for paths A and B; "enterprise AI strategy practitioner…" for path C; "enablement lead with commissioning depth across applied- and agentic-engineer peer teams" for path D). Names one credential maximum.
2. **The Licenses & Certifications section specification** — the two to five credentials that should appear in LinkedIn's indexed-for-search section. Each named with the full issuer name as the recruiter will type it into search; the credential's date; a short description if the credential is less well-known. Note any credentials you are *removing* from LinkedIn as part of this update (lapsed, superseded, or off-path).
3. **The Featured section specification** — three artefacts the Featured section should display for the chosen path's audience. May include a conference talk recording, a published piece of writing, an open-source repository, or a specific board-ready scorecard anonymised. Each with a one-sentence rationale for inclusion.

Approximately 250–350 words total.

### Part D — the public-facing bio rendering

Produce three bio variants, each for a different venue:

1. **The conference-session bio** — approximately 40–70 words. Two to four sentences; names the lead's role and organisation; names one credential as modifier; names one public artefact (the Exercise 04 talk if relevant, a published piece if applicable). The audience is industry peers.
2. **The byline bio** — one sentence, approximately 15–25 words. The "about the author" line at the end of a published piece. Name the role and organisation; one credential or public affiliation maximum.
3. **The podcast / interview bio** — approximately 60–100 words. Used by hosts for the show-notes introduction. Names the lead's role, the two or three surfaces the interview is likely to cover, the credential that signals the lead's authority on those surfaces. Slightly longer than the conference bio because the podcast host reads it live.

### Part E — the discipline check

Three short paragraphs applying Chapter 04's three disciplines to the renderings produced:

- **Discipline 1 (partner-held credential stays with the partner).** Confirm no credential in Parts B–D is one held by a partner but not by the lead. If a credential was almost included on the mistaken basis of partnership, name it and the correction.
- **Discipline 2 (expired or stale credential renewed or removed).** List any credential in the Part A snapshot that is lapsed and flag the specific action taken (removed from Parts B–D; scheduled for renewal in [specific month]).
- **Discipline 3 (aspirational credential not on the headline).** List any credential the lead is studying for but has not obtained. Confirm it does not appear in Parts B–D; name it in the preparation plan for the next cycle.

Approximately 150–200 words total.

## Starter guidance

- The hardest trade-off is which credentials to leave off. The temptation to list every credential held — especially on LinkedIn where the licenses section is indexed for search — is strong. Resist. The Chapter 04 failure-mode-1 (credential stack) is the specific drift the discipline check in Part E is designed to catch.
- For path A / B leads with AIGP but no Lead Implementer, do not render Lead Implementer as "studying." Render AIGP with full context and name the Lead Implementer ambition in Exercise 04's preparation plan.
- For path C leads with no executive-education program, the About paragraph has to carry the enterprise-AI-strategy framing without a credential anchor. This is legitimate; the Exercise 04 plan will name the executive-education program as a preparation move.
- For path D leads, the technical credential is often the hardest to choose. A cloud-vendor AI service certification, a specific technology certification (Databricks, Snowflake, HuggingFace), or an open-source maintainer badge all carry different signals. Choose the one that matches the engineering surface the lead actually operates against, not the most recognised credential in general.
- Where a credential date, issuing-body naming convention, or exam-code is uncertain, mark it `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` rather than guessing. The CV and LinkedIn surfaces are read by people who will recognise an incorrect credential name.

## Deliverables

- `portfolio-snapshot.md` — the Part A inventory.
- `cv-rendering.md` — the Part B role summary, credentials section, and two experience bullets.
- `linkedin-rendering.md` — the Part C About opening, Licenses section specification, Featured section specification.
- `bio-variants.md` — the Part D three bio variants.
- `discipline-check.md` — the Part E three-discipline check.
- A short cover note (3–5 sentences) stating the chosen path for the renderings, the credential most prominently foregrounded, and one credential deliberately left off the headline despite being held.

## Acceptance criteria

- The Part A snapshot is honest — no credential is listed as "held" that is not actually held.
- The CV role summary paragraph is three to four sentences and signals the chosen path without naming the path itself.
- The CV credentials section lists three to five credentials with specific operating-use context for each.
- The CV experience bullets show the credential's operating depth — not "AIGP certified" but "authored policy stack against AIGP body of knowledge's lifecycle framing."
- The LinkedIn About first paragraph opens with path-signalling framing and names at most one credential inline.
- The LinkedIn Licenses specification names the credentials with the full issuer name as a recruiter would search.
- All three bio variants in Part D are of distinct lengths and distinct audiences; they are not interchangeable.
- The bio variants together name no more than two credentials across the three — the discipline from Chapter 04 is held.
- Part E's discipline check confirms each of the three disciplines is applied; any deviation is named with specific reasoning.
- `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` appears wherever a credential naming convention or date is uncertain. No guessed or paraphrased credential names.
- The cover note names the chosen path, the foregrounded credential, and the one left off.

## Stretch goals

- **Produce the sister path renderings.** A set of CV / LinkedIn / bio renderings for a different path. Comparing the two sets side by side shows how the same portfolio reads differently to the two audiences; the comparison is calibration for the quarterly external-surface review.
- **Author the LinkedIn Featured section curation plan.** A rolling plan for what sits in the Featured section across the next four quarters. Specifically: what rotates in when the conference talk is delivered, what rotates in when the piece of writing is published, what rotates out when it becomes stale.
- **Author the recruiter-outreach pre-read.** Two paragraphs the lead would send to a recruiter who has made an initial approach, calibrated to the chosen path. The pre-read cites the portfolio renderings rather than attaching them; it is the test of whether the renderings are legible without being read.

## Why this exercise matters

The portfolio is the fastest-reading surface of the lead's positioning. A recruiter screens on LinkedIn in forty-five seconds; a hiring committee reads the CV's credentials section before the role summary. A lead whose portfolio reads as "generalist with many credentials" will under-perform a lead whose portfolio reads as "specific position with the right credentials for that position" — even if the two leads have the same operating depth. The rendering is the surface; the exercise is where the rendering is made deliberate, not accidental.
