# Exercise 01 — Exec Communication Story Outline

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours
**Format:** story outline at three artefact lengths, Markdown bundle
**Grounding chapters:** [01 — The Exec-Communication Story That Ties the Track Together](../01-the-exec-communication-story-that-ties-the-track-together.md). Prior reading: [mod-106 Chapter 02](../../mod-106-outcome-measurement-and-executive-reporting/02-authoring-the-executive-scorecard-weekly-monthly-quarterly.md) (misses-first discipline), [mod-106 Chapter 03](../../mod-106-outcome-measurement-and-executive-reporting/03-writing-for-executives-board-updates-lt-briefings-strategic-memos.md) (BLUF + confidence bands), [mod-111 Chapter 05](../../mod-111-ai-coe-operations-vendor-management-and-cross-functional-leadership/05-the-quarterly-cross-functional-operating-ritual.md) (the audiences the story lands in).

## Objective

Author the exec-communication story that ties the eleven prior modules into one coherent narrative, rendered at the three artefact lengths from Chapter 01 — the thirty-second elevator, the one-page memo, and the fifteen-minute talk. The output is a single story told in three compressions, each one specific enough to carry its own audience without translation.

The story is personal in the sense that the lead's voice has to show in it; the evidence is organisational. If you are using your own lead record, do so. If you do not have a two-year record to draw from, use a worked-example lead against one of the organisations below (same four as in [mod-111 Exercise 01](../../mod-111-ai-coe-operations-vendor-management-and-cross-functional-leadership/exercises/exercise-01-coe-service-catalog-and-intake-cadence.md)) — carry the same organisation through Exercises 01–04 so the four exercises reinforce each other.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter 01 of this module read.
- [mod-106 Chapters 02 and 03](../../mod-106-outcome-measurement-and-executive-reporting/README.md) read as the exec-communication craft the story inherits.
- A chosen lead record — either your own or a worked-example lead against one of these organisations (carry through Exercises 01–04):
  - A 4,000-person professional-services consultancy running a federated CoE pattern — one hub team at HQ plus embedded enablement partners in the four largest practice areas, with high consultant / contractor density.
  - An 8,000-person regional health system running a hub-and-spoke CoE pattern — one central CoE serving the hospital-group corporate centre plus five regional spoke leads embedded in large hospitals, all under HIPAA.
  - A 12,000-person bank running a federated CoE pattern — enterprise CoE at corporate plus BU-embedded leads in retail banking, commercial lending, wealth management, and risk, all under SR 11-7 model-risk-management framing.
  - A 2,500-person ed-tech vendor running a hub CoE pattern — one central team serving all functions, with K-12 regulation (FERPA, COPPA) framing the data-handling posture.
- One identified next-step audience — the specific role of the primary reader the story is being told to. One of: a CAIO at the current organisation, a board AI committee chair, the hiring committee for `head-of-ai-governance`, the hiring committee for a next-level role elsewhere. The audience choice shapes Move 2's three-claim selection.

## Problem statement

The lead is about to walk into a sixty-minute meeting with the chosen audience. The meeting will cover the enablement program's state, its direction, and one named ask from the lead. The lead has one page in hand and one elevator moment before the page is opened. They also have a fifteen-minute slot at an upcoming conference where the same story is the opening. One story, three compressions, each compression consistent with the others and specific to its audience.

## Requirements

### Part A — the surface inventory (pre-work)

Before authoring the story, produce a one-page surface inventory. For each of the eleven source modules (mod-102 through mod-111 plus mod-111 Chapter 07), name in one sentence what the chosen lead's operating record proved on that surface. Use the format from [Chapter 01](../01-the-exec-communication-story-that-ties-the-track-together.md) — a flat claim followed by the specific evidence artefact. If a surface's evidence is weak or missing in the chosen record, say so explicitly — do not pad. Approximately 350–500 words.

### Part B — the thirty-second elevator version

The sixty-word version. Exactly one sentence per move; four sentences total. The elevator version is the test case for the longer versions — if the three-claim selection, the one gap, and the one ask cannot fit here, the longer versions will drift.

Produce three elevator variants, each calibrated for a different audience:

- Variant 1: for the CAIO of the chosen organisation.
- Variant 2: for an external next-step hiring committee (specify which of the four paths you are targeting).
- Variant 3: for a conference-session opener — the audience is industry peers, not the hiring committee.

Note the specific differences across the three variants; they should not be interchangeable.

### Part C — the one-page memo version

Approximately 400 words, four short paragraphs matching the four moves, formatted in Markdown. Specifically:

- Paragraph 1 opens with the organisational problem statement sentence; names the specific exposure the chosen organisation carried.
- Paragraph 2 names three evidence-backed claims — each with the specific evidence surface (not "we rolled out AI tools" but "we ran three selection decisions through the three-stage framework, each with pilot-to-rollout commit records available in the decision log"). Attach a confidence band or sample-size qualifier to the quantitative claims.
- Paragraph 3 names one target that missed, one capability the program does not yet have, one risk being carried knowingly. Flat statements; no softening language. Each miss carries a diagnosis sentence that names a specific mechanism, not a generic explanation.
- Paragraph 4 names the ask — one decision, one alignment, or one awareness, as specified in Chapter 01. The confidence attaches to the ask; the decision date is named.

### Part D — the fifteen-minute talk outline

An outline, not a full script. Three to four minutes per move. For each move, name:

- The key sentence the audience is meant to carry out of that move.
- The one worked example the move hangs on (not three; one).
- The visual or demonstration surface (slide concept, artefact to hold up, scorecard to render on screen).
- The transition sentence into the next move.

Approximately 600–800 words total. The talk outline should stand alone — a colleague could rehearse against it.

### Part E — the hostile-reading pre-mortem

Three sentences each on the three failure modes from Chapter 01, applied to the specific story authored:

- Where the story might not reconcile to the artefacts if a hostile reader asks to see them. Name the specific artefact that would be pulled first.
- Where the misses in Move 3 might read as too soft or too dramatic. Name which miss, and the specific calibration you chose.
- Where the ask in Move 4 might read as a wish list. Name why the single ask you chose is the right one to foreground and what you specifically dropped from the ask slate.

## Starter guidance

- The three-claim selection in Move 2 is where most of the authorship time goes. Draft two or three candidate three-claim sets before committing; the "right" set is usually the one that pre-empts the audience's most likely objection, not the one that leads with the strongest number.
- Do not open with "AI is accelerating." Any sentence that could appear in a McKinsey headline is a sentence that could appear in any lead's story; the opening has to be organisation-specific and consequence-specific or the story loses the room.
- The miss in Move 3 is the part leads most often soften. Write the honest version; then re-write once to remove hedging words ("somewhat," "a bit," "we are still working on"). The hedging words are theatre; the flat version is the one that carries the room.
- Attach confidence bands to the quantitative claims in Move 2. "22%" alone is a claim; "22%, 90% CI 14–29%, pilot cohort n=180" is an auditable claim. The specificity is defence, not noise.
- The ask in Move 4 is one thing. If you find yourself writing "and also," cut. Everything after "and also" belongs in the quarterly review, not in the sixty-minute meeting.
- Where an organisational fact you need is unknown (specific scorecard numbers for a worked example, specific BU names, specific budget figures), use `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` inline. Do not invent numbers. If using your own record, cite the record; do not make specific numbers up to make the story more impressive.

## Deliverables

- `surface-inventory.md` — the one-page inventory from Part A.
- `elevator-versions.md` — the three sixty-word variants from Part B with the note on their differences.
- `memo-version.md` — the one-page memo from Part C.
- `talk-outline.md` — the fifteen-minute talk outline from Part D.
- `hostile-reading-premortem.md` — the three-failure-mode pre-mortem from Part E.
- A short cover note (3–5 sentences) stating the chosen audience for Part C, the chosen path for the external variant in Part B, and the hardest move to write across the exercise.

## Acceptance criteria

- All three compressions (elevator, memo, talk) carry the same story — a reader of all three recognises the organisational problem, the three claims, the misses, and the ask as consistent across the three.
- The three elevator variants differ specifically for their audiences; a reader of all three can name what changes from one to the next.
- Move 2's three claims each cite a specific evidence surface; no claim is unattributed.
- Move 2's quantitative claims carry confidence bands or sample-size qualifiers.
- Move 3's misses are stated flatly without hedging words; each miss carries a specific-mechanism diagnosis sentence.
- Move 4 names exactly one ask with a specific decision date; no "and also."
- The talk outline per move names a key sentence, one worked example, a visual surface, and a transition sentence — all four present for each of the four moves.
- The hostile-reading pre-mortem names a specific artefact for failure mode 1, a specific miss for failure mode 2, and a specific dropped ask for failure mode 3.
- `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` appears wherever an organisational fact is missing. No invented numbers.
- The cover note names the audience, the path, and the hardest move with one sentence of reasoning each.

## Stretch goals

- **Author the sister version for a different path.** The memo and the elevator-variant-2 use one of the four paths' audiences. Produce a second memo and a second elevator-variant-2 for a different path. The exercise is to feel how the same operating evidence reads differently to the two audiences.
- **Produce a Q&A anticipation list.** Five questions the audience is most likely to ask after the fifteen-minute talk, each with a two-sentence answer you would give if the question lands. The list is calibrated for the specific audience chosen in Part C.
- **Produce a "what I would delete" annotation.** On the memo version, annotate the two or three sentences you would delete if the memo had to be cut to 250 words. The annotation forces the lead to identify what is load-bearing vs. what is scaffolding.

## Why this exercise matters

The exec-communication story is the single artefact that most distinguishes a lead who will step up from a lead who will plateau. The operating evidence is roughly comparable across level-45 leads — most have run the operating loop, co-authored the policy stack, published the scorecard. What differs is whether the lead can carry the two years of evidence into a sixty-minute meeting and have the room acting on an ask by minute fifty-five. Producing the story at three compressions is calibration for doing so in the actual meeting; the one-page memo is the rehearsal.
