# Exercise 02 — Next Step Ladder Decision Worked Example

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours
**Format:** personal decision memo, Markdown
**Grounding chapters:** [02 — Choosing the Next Step on the Ladder](../02-choosing-the-next-step-on-the-ladder.md); supporting reads from [06 — The Decision Points That Separate This Role from Adjacent Tracks](../06-the-decision-points-that-separate-this-role-from-adjacent-tracks.md). Prior reading: [mod-101 Chapter 04](../../mod-101-role-and-the-org-wide-enablement-operating-model/04-handoffs-with-adjacent-roles.md) (the adjacent roles named).

## Objective

Run Chapter 02's four-factor sequenced decision framework end to end and produce the one-page decision memo that will anchor the preparation window. The memo is a personal artefact. It does not ship externally; it does not go to the sponsor; it lives in the lead's own notes and is re-read quarterly. Its quality lies in honesty, not polish.

## Prerequisites

- Chapters 02 and 06 of this module read.
- [mod-101 Chapter 04](../../mod-101-role-and-the-org-wide-enablement-operating-model/04-handoffs-with-adjacent-roles.md) read as the adjacent-roles map.
- The chosen organisation from [Exercise 01](exercise-01-exec-communication-story-outline.md) carried through, or your own current organisation if you are using your own record. The decision is specific to an organisation shape.
- Two to four hours of uninterrupted thinking time. The exercise is explicitly personal; it cannot be run well in the operational interrupts of a working week. Run it in a focused block — a Saturday morning, a long travel evening, a planned retreat half-day.

## Problem statement

The lead has been in the level-45 role long enough that the operating loop is predictable, the scorecard is credible, and at least two of the four adjacent next-step paths are plausibly available. Opportunity is arriving from multiple directions and the lead is deferring the decision one quarter at a time. This exercise forces the deferral to end. The lead works through the four factors from Chapter 02 honestly, names one path, and commits to the preparation window.

The decision is not permanent. It is the current-cycle decision; it will be re-read quarterly. But the decision has to be made before the preparation window can start compounding.

## Requirements

The memo is one page — approximately 500–700 words — with the five sections named below. Short sentences; no padding. The honesty of the memo is its quality criterion.

### Section 1 — the honest read on factor 1 (energy and skill)

Approximately 100–150 words.

Name the two or three surfaces of the current role that you most look forward to on Monday morning. Cite specific evidence from the last four quarters — not "I enjoy policy" but "I am the first to arrive at the Tuesday redline session with notes, and the quarterly standards-reading brief I authored for the AI council is the work I re-read for pleasure."

Name the two or three surfaces that drain you. Same evidence standard. The honest read on what drains is harder than the honest read on what energises; the second is where most leads short-change the exercise.

Separate what you are *best at* from what you are *energised by*. Chapter 02 warns that the two are not the same; this is the section where the distinction has to be made.

### Section 2 — the partnership map on factor 2

Approximately 100–150 words.

Name the three deepest standing partnerships from the quarterly ritual (or from the operating week, if the ritual has not yet produced the full four surfaces). For each:

- The partner's role and name (if the memo is personal; title alone if it is a worked example).
- The artefacts you have co-produced in the last four quarters — specific documents, not "we have worked together."
- The partner's apparent orientation — are they advocating for you to step up, are they holding you in place, are they indifferent, is their own next-step move the gating factor for yours.

The partnership map often points to the natural next step; name the natural step the map suggests even if you plan to argue against it in Section 5.

### Section 3 — the organisational shape on factor 3

Approximately 100–150 words.

Walk the four paths against the current organisation. For each:

- Does the role exist internally at the correct altitude? If it exists at title only (a "CAIO" who is really a CIO deputy, a "senior architect" who is really a senior analyst), say so.
- Is the role currently filled? If filled, is the incumbent likely to move inside the preparation window?
- Would an external search be the better pattern for this path from this organisation?

Name the paths that are available inside the organisation and the paths that require an external move. Note that an external move is a different preparation window (heavier weight on public artefacts; different sponsor-network work).

### Section 4 — the life-stage read on factor 4

Approximately 75–100 words.

The private section. Not for circulation under any circumstances; the memo is internal to the lead's own notes.

Name the life-stage inputs that are real: travel capacity, caregiving commitments, geographic ties, partner's career constraints, health posture, financial requirements, planned life changes in the preparation window. Rate each as a constraint (hard), a preference (soft), or an opportunity (positive signal). A life-stage input that rules out path C's travel load, or path D's on-call culture, or path A's regulator-meeting rhythm is a real input to the decision.

The anti-pattern this section guards against is the memo that pretends the life-stage inputs do not exist. A decision made against pretended-away constraints is a decision that unravels in year three.

### Section 5 — the decision itself

Approximately 100–150 words.

Name one path as the chosen next step. Not two; not "A or C depending on signal." One.

Two sentences on why — the primary factor from the four above that drove the choice, and the specific counter-pull you declined to follow. "I am choosing path B because factor 1 and factor 2 both pointed there and factor 3 confirms the architect role exists internally; I am declining path C despite factor 3's opening because factor 1's energy read was honest and the executive writing was a drain."

Name the horizon — 18 months, 24 months, 36 months, "open to signal in year 1 then committed." The horizon is not a binding commitment; it is a planning assumption.

Name one signal that would legitimately shift the decision. Not many; one. The quarterly re-read checks for this specific signal.

## Starter guidance

- The honest read on factor 1 is the single most common failure mode of this exercise. Leads tend to cite what they are praised for (because the praise has evidence attached) rather than what energises them (where the evidence is subjective). Run factor 1 with the evidence of time use, not the evidence of outcomes: *what work pulls me to the desk; what work I postpone*.
- The partnership map is often the most predictive single factor. The partner whose work has become the lead's primary partner surface is usually the partner whose role shape is the natural next step. Pay attention to the partner map even when the path it points to is not the one you were planning on.
- The organisational shape is the factor most likely to change without the lead's input. An acquisition, a reorg, a sponsor rotation, a sitting incumbent's resignation can open or close a path inside a single quarter. Note current state in the memo; expect the state to shift.
- The life-stage read is private. It also has to be honest. A lead writing this memo against their current partner's professional preferences without telling their partner is producing a decision memo that will not survive year one. If this section is hard to write honestly alone, write it after a conversation with the partner — but write it.
- The decision does not have to be bold. "Stay at level 45 another three years and deepen" is a legitimate decision and this memo accommodates it — Section 5 then names "none of the four" and commits to the stay-and-deepen pattern. The exercise is to make the decision, whatever it is, explicit.
- Where a factor read depends on information you do not have (specific sponsor orientation, specific internal role availability), use `<!-- needs-information: ... -->` inline. The follow-up on the open items is quarter-one work.

## Deliverables

- `decision-memo.md` — the one-page memo with the five sections.
- A short cover note (3–5 sentences) stating the factor that drove the decision most heavily, the factor that was hardest to read honestly, and the one next-quarter follow-up the memo surfaced.

## Acceptance criteria

- The memo is approximately 500–700 words with all five sections present.
- Each section cites specific evidence, not generic claim. Section 1's energy read names specific work; Section 2's partnerships name specific artefacts; Section 3's organisational shape names specific role status; Section 4's life-stage inputs are named concretely.
- Section 1 distinguishes "best at" from "energised by" with specific evidence for each.
- Section 2 names three partnerships with artefacts and partner orientation for each.
- Section 3 walks all four paths; it does not skip the paths the lead has already ruled out.
- Section 4 is present. A memo that omits this section is a memo that has deferred the hardest factor.
- Section 5 names exactly one path (or explicitly "none of the four"), with two-sentence reasoning, a horizon, and one legitimate-shift signal.
- No path is chosen by elimination alone. Section 5's two sentences name a positive reason for the chosen path, not only reasons against the others.
- `<!-- needs-information: ... -->` appears wherever an open question remains.
- The cover note names the dominant factor, the hardest factor, and the next-quarter follow-up.

## Stretch goals

- **Author the sibling counter-memo.** A second memo at the same altitude arguing for a different path. The exercise forces the lead to feel the pull of the path they declined. Running the counter-memo honestly often reveals whether the original decision was over-committed.
- **Produce the sponsor conversation script.** A 90-second monologue the lead would use to raise the decision with the current sponsor. The script is not for delivery this quarter; it is a draft that clarifies what the sponsor-conversation will have to cover when the signals arrive.
- **Produce the pre-mortem.** Imagine it is five years after the step. The path did not work out. Write three paragraphs naming the most likely reasons the path did not work. Pre-mortems surface decision regret before the decision locks in; a path with a strong pre-mortem that the lead could not mitigate is a signal to re-read Section 5.

## Why this exercise matters

A lead who has run the four-factor framework explicitly will prepare deliberately for 18 to 36 months. A lead who has deferred the decision will drift through the same window and arrive at an external approach in year three with no preparation to draw on. The difference between the two careers is this one afternoon of honest writing. The memo is personal; the compounding is real.
