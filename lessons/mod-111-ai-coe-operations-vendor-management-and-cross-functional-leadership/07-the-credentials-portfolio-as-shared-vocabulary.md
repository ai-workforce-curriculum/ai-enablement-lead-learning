# The Credentials Portfolio as Shared Vocabulary

> [mod-101 Chapter 06](../mod-101-role-and-the-org-wide-enablement-operating-model/06-credentials-and-the-90-day-entry-plan.md) named the credentials the role meets at entry — AIGP, ATD, Prosci. Nine modules on, the picture is richer: the quarterly ritual from [Chapter 05](05-the-quarterly-cross-functional-operating-ritual.md) crosses the role through four specialised partner worlds (governance, L&D, change management, program management), each with its own standing vocabulary. An enablement lead who moves across these surfaces without the shared language becomes the role's own bottleneck — every cross-functional conversation routes through the lead because the lead is the only person who holds the translation. This chapter positions the credentials portfolio deliberately: not as a stack of certifications to accumulate, but as the shared vocabulary that keeps the operating ritual from becoming a bottleneck.

## Why "portfolio" and not "stack"

A stack is a thing you build in order. A portfolio is a set of positions you hold across surfaces — each chosen for its coverage of a specific partner surface, each held at a depth calibrated for the role's purposes, each refreshed on a cadence. The portfolio framing matters because the credentials are tools of cross-functional legibility, not personal achievements:

- **AIGP** is the vocabulary of the AI council (Chapter 05, Surface 1) and the governance partnership ([mod-103](../mod-103-policy-co-authoring-with-governance/README.md), [mod-109](../mod-109-ai-incident-response-and-enablement-side-rollback/README.md)).
- **ATD** is the vocabulary of the L&D partnership (Chapter 05, Surface 4; [mod-104](../mod-104-training-programs-and-ai-literacy-curriculum/README.md)).
- **Prosci** is the vocabulary of the change-management discipline (Chapter 05, Surface 3 for BU-specific resistance work; [mod-105](../mod-105-champion-networks-and-change-management/README.md)).
- **ISO/IEC 42001 lead auditor / implementer** is the vocabulary of the architect / risk seats on the council (Chapter 05, Surface 1; mod-103's standards read) and of certification-bearing conversations.
- **PMP** is the vocabulary of program-management partners across the organisation — IT, Procurement, Finance, and large-scale program sponsors (R15 at 0.35 is the program-management surface the credentials cover).

Treating these as a portfolio — chosen deliberately, held at calibrated depth — is the posture this chapter authors. Treating them as a stack to accumulate is the posture this chapter guards against.

## The five portfolio positions

### Position 1 — IAPP AIGP (AI Governance Professional)

**Issuing body:** International Association of Privacy Professionals (IAPP). Introduced 2024.

**Vocabulary it carries into the role:** risk classification, deployer vs. provider obligations, high-risk use cases and their controls, prohibited practices, lifecycle controls, model inventory, impact assessment, the EU AI Act's article architecture, NIST AI RMF's four functions, ISO/IEC 42001's management-system shape, the OECD AI principles.

**Where it is used at steady state:** the AI council's standing agenda (Chapter 05, Surface 1); the policy walking-redline with `head-of-ai-governance` and `senior-ai-governance-architect` ([mod-103 Chapter 01](../mod-103-policy-co-authoring-with-governance/01-the-co-author-posture-and-the-redline-log.md)); the incident path's governance handoff ([mod-109 Chapter 05](../mod-109-ai-incident-response-and-enablement-side-rollback/05-handoff-contracts-ai-risk-engineer-head-of-governance-secops.md)); the industry-flavour reads ([mod-108](../mod-108-industry-and-regulatory-flavours-of-enablement/README.md)) for regulated BUs.

**Depth the role needs:** conversational fluency in the full body of knowledge, operating depth in the Article 4 literacy obligation that the role owns at the deployer level ([mod-101 Chapter 05](../mod-101-role-and-the-org-wide-enablement-operating-model/05-eu-ai-act-article-4-and-the-literacy-floor.md)). The role does not need to sit the exam, though many leads do; what matters is the body of knowledge's vocabulary in active use.

**How to hold it current:** IAPP's continuing-education calendar, the annual Global Privacy Summit for connective reading, periodic re-reads of the IAPP's AI governance resource library as regulation evolves. The EU AI Act specifically moves — the obligation dates in 2025 and 2026 reshaped parts of the vocabulary; another reshape is likely as enforcement cases develop.

### Position 2 — ATD CPTD (Certified Professional in Talent Development)

**Issuing body:** Association for Talent Development (ATD). The anchor credentials are APTD (associate) and CPTD (full professional); ATD's Talent Development Capability Model is the underlying frame.

**Vocabulary it carries into the role:** instructional-design patterns (ADDIE, SAM, agile instructional design), Kirkpatrick levels of evaluation, 70-20-10 learning blend, Bloom's taxonomy, performance support, microlearning, cohort- vs. mastery-based design, the measurement-of-learning-impact literature, the operational practices of corporate L&D.

**Where it is used at steady state:** the HR / L&D partnership (Chapter 05, Surface 4; [mod-104 Chapter 08](../mod-104-training-programs-and-ai-literacy-curriculum/08-partnering-with-hr-ld-and-internal-comms.md)); the training-production agency's certification review ([Chapter 04](04-vendor-and-external-partner-management.md)); the fluency-measurement surface from [mod-104 Chapter 07](../mod-104-training-programs-and-ai-literacy-curriculum/07-measuring-fluency-growth.md).

**Depth the role needs:** conversational fluency. The enablement lead is usually not the certified holder of CPTD in the CoE — the training-curriculum lead or a senior learning designer in the operations shape is, often as a direct report or a partnership from L&D. The enablement lead's vocabulary floor is: Kirkpatrick levels, ADDIE, the 70-20-10 shape, how fluency is measured. Deep design belongs to the credentialed partner; the lead reads the partner's work with the right vocabulary.

**How to hold it current:** ATD's annual International Conference & EXPO, ATD's Research Tracking reports on AI-in-L&D, and the Josh Bersin research-and-advisory stream named in [mod-106 Chapter 04](../mod-106-outcome-measurement-and-executive-reporting/04-reading-the-empirical-ai-productivity-literature-for-method-not-numbers.md).

### Position 3 — Prosci Change Management Certification

**Issuing body:** Prosci. The flagship credential is the Prosci Change Management Certification; the underlying content is the ADKAR model (Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement) plus Prosci's organisational-change methodology.

**Vocabulary it carries into the role:** ADKAR as a diagnostic instrument, Prosci's three-phase process (prepare, manage, sustain), change-practitioner and sponsor roles, resistance-management patterns, change saturation. Kotter's 8-step process is a common sibling frame that often shows up in the same partner's toolkit ([mod-105 Chapter 06](../mod-105-champion-networks-and-change-management/06-ai-specific-resistance-patterns-adkar-and-kotter.md)).

**Where it is used at steady state:** the BU leadership reviews (Chapter 05, Surface 3) when a rollout is encountering resistance; the champion-network-health conversations from [mod-105 Chapter 07](../mod-105-champion-networks-and-change-management/07-measuring-change-management-health.md); the incident-path communication work ([mod-109 Chapter 04](../mod-109-ai-incident-response-and-enablement-side-rollback/04-the-first-ten-minutes-to-the-first-hour.md)); the resistance-pattern work on AI-specific adoption ([mod-105 Chapter 06](../mod-105-champion-networks-and-change-management/06-ai-specific-resistance-patterns-adkar-and-kotter.md)).

**Depth the role needs:** operating depth. Unlike ATD and (optionally) AIGP, the role personally benefits from being Prosci-certified — the diagnostic and intervention vocabulary is the lead's own practice tool, not just a translation surface for partners. Prosci's three-day certification is the standard; the enablement lead holds the certification personally and renews on the sponsor's recommended cadence (typically bi-annual continuing education).

**How to hold it current:** Prosci's annual research reports (the "Best Practices in Change Management" series), continuing-education modules, and the periodic re-reads as AI-specific resistance patterns evolve. The ACMP (Association of Change Management Professionals) runs a parallel certification path (CCMP) that some partners carry; conversational fluency on both frames is useful.

### Position 4 — ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Auditor / Lead Implementer

**Issuing body:** Various accredited training providers for ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (AI Management Systems standard). The two training streams are the Lead Implementer (designing and operating a 42001-compliant management system) and the Lead Auditor (conducting a conformity audit of one).

**Vocabulary it carries into the role:** AI management system clauses (particularly 4–10 and Annex A controls), the plan-do-check-act cycle applied to AI, the roles of top management, AI management system teams, the AI impact assessment, the risk-management linkages to ISO/IEC 23894, the conformity audit's evidence surface.

**Where it is used at steady state:** the AI council's standards-read agenda ([mod-103 Chapter 03](../mod-103-policy-co-authoring-with-governance/03-nist-ai-rmf-govern-and-iso-iec-42001-for-the-enablement-seat.md) is the depth); the architect / risk seats on the council (Chapter 05, Surface 1); the vendor-screening security review's standards column ([Chapter 04](04-vendor-and-external-partner-management.md)); the incident path's conformity evidence ([mod-109 Chapter 07](../mod-109-ai-incident-response-and-enablement-side-rollback/07-briefing-the-governance-council-and-supporting-article-73-reporting.md)).

**Depth the role needs:** conversational fluency on both streams, operating depth where the organisation pursues certification. For organisations that are certifying — common in regulated industries, less common elsewhere — the enablement lead is one of the people the Lead Auditor interviews during conformity audits; holding the Lead Implementer credential is often what makes that interview reproducible.

**How to hold it current:** the standard itself is updated periodically (first publication 2023; next revision cycle TBD); accredited training providers release continuing-education modules on each revision. <!-- needs-research: ISO/IEC 42001 revision cycle and date of first revision; confirm on each content cycle. -->

### Position 5 — PMP (Project Management Professional)

**Issuing body:** Project Management Institute (PMI).

**Vocabulary it carries into the role:** scope-time-cost-quality balance, stakeholder management, risk management, change control, the five process groups and ten knowledge areas, the PMBOK's current edition, agile-and-hybrid delivery frames.

**Where it is used at steady state:** program-management partnerships at enterprise scale — IT, Procurement, Finance, program sponsors in BUs ([mod-107 Chapter 04](../mod-107-budget-roi-and-build-vs-buy-at-org-scope/04-partnering-with-procurement-finance-and-it.md)); large commissioned-build operations ([mod-110](../mod-110-commissioning-internal-ai-tools-and-custom-agents/README.md)); the quarterly operating ritual's program-level shape ([Chapter 05](05-the-quarterly-cross-functional-operating-ritual.md)).

**Depth the role needs:** conversational fluency, with operating depth on the risk-management and stakeholder-management surfaces specifically. R15 (0.35, cross-functional program management, prereq-level in the sample) treats program-management practice as a prerequisite — PMP is one of the common evidence surfaces for that prerequisite. An enablement lead who does not hold PMP but has equivalent evidence (SAFe RTE / LPM, PgMP, documented program-of-programs experience) is equally positioned; the vocabulary is what counts, not the badge alone.

**How to hold it current:** PMP's PDU (professional development unit) requirement is 60 PDUs every three years, which is light. The vocabulary moves more than the certification does — the PMBOK revisions reshape the vocabulary every 4–5 years; the current-edition read is the useful discipline.

## How the portfolio is held

Not every position is held to the same depth. The chapter's framing is calibrated:

| Position | Depth | Credential shape |
|---|---|---|
| AIGP | Conversational to operating | Optional exam; body-of-knowledge read mandatory |
| ATD CPTD | Conversational | Held by a partner (training-curriculum lead or L&D counterpart) |
| Prosci | Operating | Lead holds personally; recertifies on sponsor cadence |
| ISO/IEC 42001 | Conversational to operating | Lead Implementer if the org certifies; body-of-knowledge read otherwise |
| PMP | Conversational to operating | Held personally or by documented equivalent program-management evidence |

The calibration is deliberate. The lead's own direct holdings — Prosci, usually PMP, sometimes AIGP, sometimes Lead Implementer — are the positions where operating depth is required because the lead *is* the practitioner. The partner-held positions — ATD CPTD specifically — are held at conversational depth because the lead is the translator, not the practitioner.

A lead who tries to hold all five at operating depth is a lead who is training against the role's partners rather than training with them. The portfolio works when it respects the partnership shape, not when it mirrors every partner's expertise.

## When the portfolio is read

The portfolio is read externally — on the role's CV, in the interview process, on the LinkedIn surface, in the first partner meetings. It is also read internally — in the quarterly ritual's standing surfaces, in the vendor-certification reviews from [Chapter 04](04-vendor-and-external-partner-management.md), and in the hand-offs with level-60 and level-50 roles up the ladder.

Externally the portfolio is one input to [mod-112](../mod-112-career-positioning-and-the-next-step-on-the-ladder/README.md)'s positioning work — specific combinations of credentials signal specific next steps. AIGP plus Lead Implementer signals a lead positioning toward `head-of-ai-governance` (level 60) or `senior-ai-governance-architect` (level 50). AIGP plus ATD plus Prosci signals a lead positioning toward `chief-ai-officer` (level 70) with a change-and-workforce depth. PMP plus engineering adjacency signals `ai-engineering-manager` (level 50). The portfolio signals; the lead's narrative explains.

Internally the portfolio is read at the quarterly ritual's standing surfaces:

- The AI council (Surface 1) reads the lead's AIGP / Lead Implementer vocabulary.
- The exec review (Surface 2) reads the lead's PMP / program-management vocabulary and the ROI confidence discipline from the AIGP body of knowledge's lifecycle framing.
- The BU reviews (Surface 3) read the lead's Prosci vocabulary on resistance and the ATD vocabulary on training delivery.
- The functional-partnership renewals (Surface 4) read the full portfolio — each partner reads the piece that maps to their function.

The ritual works when the vocabulary is shared. The portfolio is the discipline that keeps the vocabulary shared.

## Two drifts the portfolio prevents

### Drift 1 — The role becomes the sole translator

Without a shared vocabulary, every cross-functional conversation routes through the enablement lead. Legal talks to HR via the lead; Procurement talks to governance via the lead; L&D talks to the champion community via the lead. The lead becomes a bottleneck — every scheduling conflict becomes a program delay, every illness becomes a quarter-blocking event.

The portfolio prevents the drift by making the lead one of several people in the room who can translate, not the only one. The vendor's training sessions ([Chapter 04](04-vendor-and-external-partner-management.md)) include the policy stack in partner-fluent language. The functional-partnership renewals (Chapter 05, Surface 4) are conducted in each partner's vocabulary. The champion community's cross-team posts (Chapter 06) use the shared vocabulary liberally so that champions across BUs recognise each other's work.

### Drift 2 — The credentials become the point

The opposite drift is also failure: the lead collects credentials as personal brand-building and the operating loop is neglected. Three years in, the lead holds AIGP, CPTD, PMP, Prosci, Lead Auditor, and Lead Implementer — and the organisation's enablement scorecard has not moved. The credentials are being accumulated for external career signalling, not for operating practice.

The portfolio is the surface that catches this. A credential is held because it carries a specific partner-surface vocabulary. If the vocabulary is not being exercised inside the ritual, the credential is a trophy and the acquiring time was misspent. The honest read against the portfolio each year — *which position am I actually using in the operating loop, and which am I holding out of ambition only* — is what prevents the drift.

## Summary

- **The credentials portfolio is shared vocabulary, not a stack of certifications.** It is held as a portfolio because each position maps to a specific partner surface in the operating ritual.
- **Five portfolio positions:** AIGP for governance, ATD CPTD for L&D, Prosci for change management, ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Implementer / Lead Auditor for standards, PMP for program management. Each is calibrated to a specific depth — some held personally at operating depth, some held at conversational depth with a credentialed partner.
- **The portfolio is read externally for career signalling** ([mod-112](../mod-112-career-positioning-and-the-next-step-on-the-ladder/README.md)) and **internally at the quarterly ritual's four surfaces** (Chapter 05). Each surface reads the piece that maps to its own function.
- **Two drifts the portfolio prevents:** the lead becoming the sole translator across functions, and the credentials becoming the point. The annual honest read against the portfolio is the discipline that catches both.
