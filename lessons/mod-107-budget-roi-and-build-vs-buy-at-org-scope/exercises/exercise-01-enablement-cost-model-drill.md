# Exercise 01 — Enablement Cost Model Drill

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours
**Format:** cost model (Markdown table or spreadsheet) plus a one-page methodology appendix
**Grounding chapters:** [01 — The Enablement Cost Model at Org Scope](../01-the-enablement-cost-model-at-org-scope.md), [04 — Partnering with Procurement, Finance, and IT](../04-partnering-with-procurement-finance-and-it.md)

## Objective

Produce a full annual enablement cost model for one chosen organization, using the six-family taxonomy from [Chapter 01](../01-the-enablement-cost-model-at-org-scope.md). The deliverable is the model itself (every family populated, every line attributed to an authoritative source, materiality threshold stated), a methodology appendix that a Finance partner could sign off on, and a one-paragraph defence against each of the five named undercounts from [Chapter 01](../01-the-enablement-cost-model-at-org-scope.md).

The model is the denominator the ROI narrative from [mod-106 Chapter 05](../../mod-106-outcome-measurement-and-executive-reporting/05-partnering-with-finance-on-cost-model-and-roi.md) and the projections in Exercise 02 of this module both depend on; getting it complete and defensible is the gate.

## Prerequisites

- [Chapter 01](../01-the-enablement-cost-model-at-org-scope.md) read, with the six families, the sourcing map, and the five named undercounts close enough to cite without the chapter open.
- [Chapter 04](../04-partnering-with-procurement-finance-and-it.md) read for the shape of the Procurement, Finance, and IT partnerships the sourcing map depends on.
- [mod-106 Chapter 05](../../mod-106-outcome-measurement-and-executive-reporting/05-partnering-with-finance-on-cost-model-and-roi.md) read; the fully-loaded FTE multiplier question is load-bearing.
- One chosen organization — fictional-but-realistic is fine; the organization must have at least two business units, at least two task-category rollouts in production, and at least one vendor renewal in the next twelve months. Carrying the same organization across Exercises 01, 02, 03, and 05 produces a coherent bundle.

## Problem statement

Your Finance partner has asked you to produce the first joint-signed annual enablement cost model for the fiscal year beginning in six weeks. The previous year's cost model reported only Family 1 (tool licenses) and a partial Family 2 (CoE team salaries only, not fully-loaded). The CFO flagged the model as "incomplete" in the last quarterly review; Finance has agreed to co-build the new model if you bring a defensible six-family structure.

You have two hours (this exercise) to produce the first full draft that you will take into the Finance working session. The draft must be complete across all six families, grounded in a stated sourcing map, and defended against the five named undercounts from [Chapter 01](../01-the-enablement-cost-model-at-org-scope.md).

## Requirements

### Part A — the cost model

Produce a cost model with:

- **Family 1 — Tool licenses.** Itemized by tool. Include enterprise seat subscriptions (with contracted-seat counts, per-seat rate, annualized total), usage-based charges (at trailing-twelve-month average or current run-rate, stated), premium add-ons and tiers (itemized), true-up and over-use charges (if applicable), pilot and POC costs (while running).
- **Family 2 — Enablement-program direct spend.** CoE team fully-loaded headcount (with roles, fully-loaded multiplier stated), external services and consulting, enablement tooling (LMS, authoring, surveys), content production, travel and events.
- **Family 3 — Training production and delivery.** Curriculum development labor, SME time (attested, with rate), delivery labor, learning-platform and content licensing, assessment and credentialing.
- **Family 4 — Embedded enablement partner FTE.** BU-embedded partners (with percentage-of-time allocations), champion network operator time, shadow enablement time (attested), governance partner time.
- **Family 5 — Champion-network operating cost.** Recognition and rewards, champion training and development, community infrastructure, dedicated champion-coordinator role if applicable.
- **Family 6 — Downstream support, risk, operating.** IT support load, security review and monitoring, legal / compliance review, incident-response trailing cost, manager change-management load, operator learning time.

Each line carries: the dollar value (annualized), the family code, the authoritative source (Family 1: vendor invoice + procurement ledger; Family 4: HR/Workforce Planning + BU attestation; etc.), and the reconciliation cadence.

The total annual denominator lands at the bottom of the model with a composition chart or table showing the family-level breakdown.

### Part B — the methodology appendix

A one-page document that a Finance partner would co-sign. Covers:

- **The fully-loaded FTE multiplier.** The organization's standard; stated explicitly; cited to whoever owns the number.
- **The materiality threshold.** The dollar threshold below which individual lines are collapsed into "other / small lines" within their family. Typical: 0.5% of total denominator. Stated with rationale.
- **The attestation convention.** How attested lines (SME time, shadow enablement, manager change-management load) are collected; sign-off chain (contributor's manager, not contributor alone); refresh cadence (quarterly attestation per Chapter 01).
- **The AI-incremental cost rule.** How pre-existing spend is separated from AI-incremental spend (particularly for platform add-ons that sit on existing SaaS contracts). Stated explicitly with worked examples.
- **The vendor-invoice recognition rule.** How variable charges are recognized to the period they belong to (not the period the invoice landed), and how true-ups are reconciled. Stated with the recognition convention.

### Part C — the five-undercount defence

For each of the five named undercounts from [Chapter 01](../01-the-enablement-cost-model-at-org-scope.md), one paragraph (3-5 sentences) in the methodology appendix that explicitly addresses:

1. "Where is the champion time?" — point to the Family 4 and Family 5 lines; state the attribution convention.
2. "Where is the operator learning time?" — point to the Family 6 line; state how the hours per adoption cycle × cohort size × fully-loaded rate calculation was built.
3. "Where is the help-desk cost?" — point to the Family 6 line; state the AI-tag sourcing or the sampled-estimate fallback.
4. "Why is Family 2 so small?" — if Family 2 has been expanded to fully-loaded, confirm the multiplier; if there are structural reasons it is small (lean CoE, outsourced function), state them.
5. "Where is the pre-existing cost separated from the AI-incremental cost?" — point to the AI-incremental cost rule in the methodology appendix; state one worked example.

## Starter guidance

- The sketch in [Chapter 01](../01-the-enablement-cost-model-at-org-scope.md) is illustrative, not prescriptive — the shape and family composition are the guide, not the specific numbers. Your organization will have its own composition.
- Numbers should be realistic relative to the organization's scale. A 2,000-person organization's enablement program is not a 50,000-person organization's enablement program. If your tool-license line (Family 1) is 95% of the total model, you have under-counted Families 2-6; this is the single most common calibration failure.
- The hardest families to populate are Family 4 (embedded FTE) and Family 6 (downstream). Start them with order-of-magnitude estimates, mark them as attested, and name in the methodology appendix that these are the lines most likely to be refined in the Finance working session. This is better than leaving them blank; a working session with Finance works better from an imperfect starting point than from a half-model.
- The attested lines are not "guesses" — they are structured estimates signed off by the time-contributor's manager per the attestation convention. The wording matters; "attested at X hours per quarter via the Q2 attestation cycle" reads differently than "estimated at X".
- The AI-incremental cost rule is the single clause that most often shocks a reviewer. Microsoft 365 licenses that existed before Copilot is added are not AI-program cost; the incremental Copilot license is. Write the rule explicitly with a worked example; this prevents the conversation where the entire M365 bill gets dragged into the denominator.
- The materiality threshold protects against over-engineering. Below 0.5% of total, roll lines up. The materiality threshold is a methodology decision co-authored with Finance in the working session; a stated threshold is defensible, an unstated one is drift.

## Deliverables

- `cost-model.md` (or `.xlsx`) containing the six-family model with every family populated, lines attributed to sources, and the annual denominator total.
- `methodology-appendix.md` containing the methodology appendix (Part B) and the five-undercount defence (Part C).

## Acceptance criteria

- Every one of the six families has at least three line items; no family is a single-line placeholder.
- The fully-loaded FTE multiplier is stated in the methodology appendix and applied consistently across Families 2, 3, 4, and relevant Family 6 lines.
- The materiality threshold is stated as a specific percentage of total denominator, with rationale.
- The attestation convention is stated with the sign-off chain; attested lines in the model carry a flag indicating they are attested.
- The AI-incremental cost rule is stated with at least one worked example (pre-existing cost identified, AI-incremental cost identified, line in model matches).
- Each of the five named undercounts has its own paragraph in the methodology appendix; none of the paragraphs is a deflection or a "we will address this later".
- The total annual denominator is a single number at the bottom of the model with the family composition (either as a table, a pie chart, or a stacked bar); Family 1 is not more than 60% of the total unless a structural reason is stated.
- The model names the specific organization (even if fictional) by scale and BU composition — not a generic "mid-sized organization" placeholder.

## Stretch goals

- Produce the one-page executive summary of the cost model you would send to the CFO ahead of the Finance working session. The BLUF structure from [mod-106 Chapter 03](../../mod-106-outcome-measurement-and-executive-reporting/03-writing-for-executives-board-updates-lt-briefings-strategic-memos.md) applies: lead with the total denominator and the biggest shift from last year's model, not with the family detail.
- Build a three-year forecast of the cost model, carrying contractual uplift on Family 1, planned CoE growth on Family 2, and planned expansion on Family 4. The forecast is the input to Exercise 02's ROI projection; align the horizon.
- Reverse-engineer last year's incomplete cost model into this year's six-family structure; produce a short written narrative on what moved, what was newly discovered, and what portion of the increase is methodology (not real growth). This is the narrative your Finance partner will ask for; writing it now is the credibility move.

## Why this exercise matters

The cost model is the single most-ignored artifact in the enablement program's operating set until the first quarter it is attacked. An incomplete cost model produces an ROI number that will not survive a Finance challenge ([mod-106 Chapter 05](../../mod-106-outcome-measurement-and-executive-reporting/05-partnering-with-finance-on-cost-model-and-roi.md)); a cost model that under-counts Family 4 and Family 6 by the typical margin produces a reported denominator that is less than half of reality. The model is where the enablement program establishes itself as a serious operating counterparty to Finance — or doesn't. This exercise is the first draft of that artifact for your chosen organization.
