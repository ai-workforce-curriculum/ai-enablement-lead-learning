# Exercise 05 — 90-Day Entry Plan

**Estimated effort:** 2.5 hours
**Format:** week-by-week plan + named-artifact milestones + honest risk register
**Grounding chapters:** [06 — Credentials, Shared Vocabulary, and the 90-Day Entry Plan](../06-credentials-and-the-90-day-entry-plan.md), plus Chapters 01–05

## Objective

Write the 90-day entry plan you would actually run the first time you hold the AI Enablement Lead title at your chosen organization. The plan ties together the scope contract (Exercise 01), the CoE shape (Exercise 02), the handoff map (Exercise 03), and the Article 4 literacy baseline (Exercise 04) into one week-by-week sequence with named artifacts and honest risks.

It is not a fantasy. It is a plan a sponsor and a boss can read, redline, and hold you to.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter 06 read.
- Exercises 01–04 completed (or at minimum, the scope contract, CoE shape choice, and handoff map drafted).
- A clear picture of who your boss, your executive sponsor, and your governance counterpart are in the chosen organization (real names or roles — "the CAIO" is enough).

## Problem statement

Your start date is in three weeks. Your boss has asked for "the 90-day plan" before you begin. You have a clean sheet and no actual context inside the organization yet — only what you learned in the interview process and from public information. Write the plan knowing it will be revised in week 2 as you learn, but that the *shape* of what you commit to now will determine how much credibility you have at day 90.

## Requirements

### Part A — The plan (three phases, twelve weeks)

Follow the three-phase sequence from Chapter 06:

1. **Days 1–30 — Observe, meet, draft.** Week 1, 2, 3, 4 each have named activities and named counterparts. Produce the artifacts the chapter names (draft scope contract, draft CoE shape choice, landscape memo) by day 30.
2. **Days 31–60 — Align, sign, publish.** Week 5–8. Convert drafts to signed v1.0 artifacts. Open the intake path to a small invite cohort.
3. **Days 61–90 — Ship, measure, iterate.** Week 9–12. Run the operating model on real traffic. Deliver the first curriculum cohort, the first end-to-end rollout, the first executive scorecard.

For each week, write:

- **Objective** — one sentence.
- **Meetings / activities** — three to six concrete items.
- **Artifact produced or advanced** — named, versioned.
- **Risk / unknown** — one line, the thing you are watching for that week.

### Part B — The artifact milestones

A single table extracting every named artifact in Part A with its target date. Rows like:

| Artifact | Draft by | v1.0 by | Audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scope contract | Day 25 | Day 45 | Boss, governance counterpart, HR partner |
| CoE shape decision memo | Day 20 | Day 50 | Executive sponsor, executive team |
| Operating-model document | Day 42 | Day 55 | Governance, security, pilot BU leaders |
| Service catalog v0.1 | Day 50 | — | Internal CoE; not yet published to org |
| Decision-rights table | Day 42 | Day 55 | Signed by CoE / BU / Governance / Security |
| Level 1 literacy curriculum, pilot cohort | Day 70 | — | Pilot cohort |
| Article 4 evidence spine, v0 | Day 80 | — | Governance partner |
| First end-to-end rollout artifact bundle | Day 85 | — | Pilot cohort |
| First executive scorecard | Day 90 | — | Boss, sponsor, governance |

Adjust to your organization. The table should be *your* artifact milestones, not a copy of the above.

### Part C — The risk register

Four to six risks. For each:

- The risk, named.
- Likelihood (high / medium / low).
- Mitigation — one specific thing you will do (or not do) in the 90 days to reduce it.

Candidate risks to include (pick the ones that fit your context; add your own):

- The scope contract is not signed by day 45 because governance wants to re-negotiate ownership of policy.
- The CoE shape decision gets re-opened by executive-team debate in week 7.
- The first pilot BU is politically important and under-prepared; a pilot failure is visible.
- A shadow-AI incident surfaces in week 6 before the incident path is live.
- You cannot hire the Training & Curriculum Lead inside 90 days.
- The executive scorecard inputs are not measurable because baselines were never captured.

### Part D — The explicit non-goals

Three to five things you will **not** do in the 90 days. Examples:

- No flagship-tool org-wide rollout.
- No permanent hiring beyond backfills already approved.
- No championing an opinion on prohibited practices beyond the governance partner's lead.
- No commitment to a specific vendor envelope change.
- No revisit of the CoE shape before day 180.

Non-goals are as important as goals. They are the thing you point at when pressure arrives mid-quarter.

## Starter guidance

- Keep the plan grounded. "Meet five BU leaders in week 2" is a commitment you can keep. "Transform AI adoption across the organization in 90 days" is a commitment you cannot.
- Observe before you commit. The first 30 days are for listening; week 1 is not the time for the scope contract redline meeting.
- Ship something small by day 90. The point is not the output — the first rollout can be modest — the point is that the operating model has flushed real work end to end and the scorecard numbers are earned.
- Over-communicate the non-goals. The scope contract from Exercise 01 should be visible to everyone the non-goals might surprise.
- The hardest week is week 5. That is when the drafts turn into negotiated agreements and your scope contract is actively tested. Make sure your week-5 calendar has room for 1-on-1s with the people who will push hardest on scope.

## Deliverables

- `90-day-plan.md` — the twelve-week plan (Part A).
- `artifact-milestones.md` — the table (Part B). May be the bottom section of the plan file.
- `risk-register.md` — the four-to-six-row register (Part C).
- `non-goals.md` — the three-to-five explicit non-goals (Part D). May be a short appendix inside the plan file.

## Acceptance criteria

- The plan fits in four to six pages at body-text size. Longer than that and you are over-specifying; nobody runs a plan that length.
- Every week has an objective, concrete activities, a named artifact, and a named risk.
- Every artifact in the milestones table maps back to a week in the plan.
- The risk register has at least one risk you cannot fully mitigate; the mitigation names the *reduction*, not a fantasy "fully mitigated."
- The non-goals are written as commitments (first-person, present tense) and would not embarrass you if your boss forwarded them to the executive team.
- The plan would survive a read-out: a colleague who doesn't know the organization can understand the shape of your first 90 days in ten minutes.

## Stretch goals

- Write a **90-day review template** — what you'd present on day 90 to your boss and sponsor. One page. "Here is what I committed to in the plan; here is what I shipped; here is what I didn't and why; here is what the next 90 days look like." The template is its own artifact and forces the plan to be measurable.
- Build a **"day-91 posture"** paragraph — in one paragraph, what the role looks like at day 91 that it did not look like at day 1. This is the thing your sponsor will read before deciding the first budget increase or hire.
- Compare your plan to a **conservative variant** that pushes the first end-to-end rollout to day 120 instead of day 90. Decide, in writing, which variant you would propose and why.

## Why this exercise matters

The first 90 days of a new role set the tone for the next three years. An AI Enablement Lead who commits to a flagship-tool rollout in the first 90 days and misses it loses credibility before they've earned it. One who observes, aligns, ships one small thing well, and reports honestly builds the credit line that funds the harder work in year one. Writing the plan now — before the real pressure arrives — gives you a sober baseline you can edit on the ground.
