# Resources for mod-101 — Role, Scope, and the Org-Wide Enablement Operating Model

Primary sources first; secondary sources only where they add something a primary source does not cover. If a link is broken by the time you read this, search for the title on the issuer's site; URLs move more often than titles.

## Regulation and standards

- **EU AI Act — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.** Full consolidated text on EUR-Lex: <https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj>. Article 4 ("AI literacy") is the section to read repeatedly for this module. Published in the OJ 2024-07-12; Article 4 and prohibited-practices provisions apply from 2025-02-02.
- **European Commission — AI Office.** The EU body coordinating implementation of the AI Act, including provider and deployer guidance: <https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-office>. Watch for Article-4 specific guidance as member-state enforcement matures. <!-- needs-research: catalog the AI Office's published Article 4 guidance documents as of 2026-11. -->
- **NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) 1.0** and the **Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1).** Primary source: <https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework>. The Govern / Map / Measure / Manage functions structure the control vocabulary your governance partners use in the US.
- **ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Management system.** Standard page: <https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html>. The international management-system standard for AI; organizations can certify. Read the clause structure (similar to ISO/IEC 27001) to understand what an auditor expects of a management system.
- **OECD AI Principles.** Primary source: <https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles>. Intergovernmental AI principles (adopted 2019, updated 2024). Used as a reference-point for "aligned with international norms" claims.

## Credentials and bodies of knowledge

- **IAPP AIGP — AI Governance Professional.** Program page: <https://iapp.org/certify/aigp/>. Even if you do not sit the exam, review the Body of Knowledge — it is the vocabulary your governance partners use.
- **ATD — Association for Talent Development.** Capability Model: <https://www.td.org/capability-model>. CPTD and APTD credential pages are reachable from the ATD site. Your L&D partner probably uses this frame.
- **Prosci Change Management Methodology and ADKAR model.** Primary source: <https://www.prosci.com/>. The methodology is proprietary but widely referenced; most change-management partners hold Prosci certifications and speak ADKAR.
- **Kotter 8-Step Process.** Primary source: <https://www.kotterinc.com/methodology/>. Alternative / complementary frame to ADKAR; most organizations have exposure to both.

## Reports and reference material on CoE and operating models

The AI CoE pattern family is not a published standard; it is a recurring shape. The references below are the most useful general reading on how enterprises organize shared capability. Treat them as background, not authority.

- **McKinsey — The State of AI (annual Global Survey).** Operating-model shapes among AI-mature enterprises: <https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights>. Most years include a breakdown of centralized vs. hub-and-spoke vs. federated organizational shapes for AI teams.
- **Deloitte — State of Generative AI in the Enterprise (quarterly).** Enterprise operating-model practices for AI adoption: <https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/issues/trust/state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise.html>.
- **Gartner — AI Center of Excellence research.** Gartner subscribers can pull the AI CoE operating-model notes; non-subscribers can read Gartner's public blog posts on CoE structure as orientation.

## Industry and incident reference

- **AI Incident Database (AIID).** Primary source: <https://incidentdatabase.ai/>. Public catalog of AI incidents; useful as grounded material for training content, policy examples, and incident-response exercises.

## How to use these resources

- The regulation and standards section is the one you must read in the primary source — paraphrases drift, and the exact wording matters when legal or governance partners quote it.
- The credentials section is for *vocabulary*, not completion. Read the bodies of knowledge; sit the exams only if the career plan justifies it (covered in [mod-112](../mod-112-career-positioning-and-the-next-step-on-the-ladder/README.md)).
- The reports section is for orientation on market practice; do not cite them as evidence for decisions about your own organization.
- The incident databases are evidence for training and policy — use them to replace speculative "what could go wrong" with recorded "what did go wrong."
