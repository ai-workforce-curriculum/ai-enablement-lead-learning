# Credentials, Shared Vocabulary, and the 90-Day Entry Plan

> You can do the job without any of the credentials in this chapter. You cannot do the job without the vocabulary they carry. The point of naming them here is not "collect certifications" — it is to recognize the shared language your legal, HR, governance, and change-management partners already speak, so that when you walk into the room you don't need a translator.

## Why credentials, not just skills

In large organizations, cross-functional partners trust the roles you work with by the credentials their peers hold. Legal partners trust CIPM / CIPT / CIPP. HR and L&D partners trust CPTD / APTD and SHRM-SCP. Change management partners trust Prosci and ACMP. If you walk in with none of the vocabulary, you spend the first six months translating.

The credentials below are the ones with *market signal* for this role in the current cycle. Not the only ones; the ones a new AI Enablement Lead most benefits from being conversational about.

## IAPP AIGP — AI Governance Professional

**Issuing body:** International Association of Privacy Professionals (IAPP). **Introduced:** 2024.

**What it certifies:** a working knowledge of AI governance frameworks — NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, OECD AI Principles — the lifecycle of AI systems, risk-management approaches, major regulatory frameworks (the EU AI Act prominently), and the governance roles that organize the work.

**Why it matters for this role:** AIGP is the credential your governance partners are most likely to hold (or be studying for). It establishes a shared vocabulary for the terms you will spend the most time negotiating: *risk classification, deployer obligations, high-risk use cases, prohibited practices, model inventory, lifecycle controls*. Even if you do not sit the exam, read the body of knowledge; it is the vocabulary of the handoff to [`head-of-ai-governance`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/head-of-ai-governance-learning) and [`senior-ai-governance-architect`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/senior-ai-governance-architect-learning).

## ATD (Association for Talent Development) credentials

**Issuing body:** Association for Talent Development (ATD). The two anchor credentials are **APTD** (Associate Professional in Talent Development) and **CPTD** (Certified Professional in Talent Development). ATD also publishes the *Talent Development Capability Model*, which is the frame most L&D professionals use.

**What they certify:** a working knowledge of instructional design, learning technologies, assessment, change-management-in-L&D, measurement of learning impact, and the operating practices of corporate learning.

**Why it matters for this role:** your training program is a corporate L&D program with AI subject matter sitting inside it. If you design training without the L&D vocabulary — Kirkpatrick levels, ADDIE, 70-20-10, performance support, microlearning — you will design a program your L&D partners cannot plug into or measure. You are not likely to become ATD-credentialed yourself; your Training & Curriculum Lead from [Chapter 03](03-authoring-the-enablement-operating-model.md) almost certainly should be.

## Prosci Change Management Certification (and the ADKAR model)

**Issuing body:** Prosci. The flagship credential is the **Prosci Change Management Certification**; the underlying content is the **ADKAR model** (Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement).

**What it certifies:** a working knowledge of individual-change and organizational-change practice, ADKAR as a diagnostic, and Prosci's structured change-management methodology.

**Why it matters for this role:** AI adoption is a change-management problem that happens to be about AI. If you treat it as a technology problem, you fail at the human side. Prosci vocabulary is lingua franca in change-management partners' rooms. [mod-105](../mod-105-champion-networks-and-change-management/README.md) leans on this; [Kotter's 8-Step Process](https://www.kotterinc.com/methodology/) is the common alternative frame and is often in the same partner's toolkit.

## Standards your governance partners speak

Not credentials; they are documents you should be able to discuss without a glossary.

- **NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) 1.0** — US framework, voluntary, structures AI risk management around four functions (Govern, Map, Measure, Manage). Companion Generative AI Profile published 2024. The frame your US-regulated partners use.
- **ISO/IEC 42001:2023** — AI Management System standard. Audit-bearing (organizations can certify). The frame your international partners and certification-minded organizations use.
- **EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)** — covered in [Chapter 05](05-eu-ai-act-article-4-and-the-literacy-floor.md). The regulation your EU-touching partners must comply with.
- **OECD AI Principles** — intergovernmental principles adopted 2019, revised 2024. Reference point for "aligned with international norms" claims.

## General program-management and leadership credentials

Not AI-specific; still likely to help you.

- **PMP** (Project Management Professional, PMI). The lingua franca of program management at enterprise scope. Many of your cross-functional partners hold it.
- **SAFe RTE / LPM** or equivalent agile-at-scale certs. Useful if your organization runs at scale on SAFe or similar; otherwise optional.
- **Executive-education programs** (e.g., executive AI strategy programs at business schools). Not credentials per se; the *network* and the *common vocabulary* among executive peers is the value.

## The 90-day entry plan

This is a plan to run the first time you hold the title. It assumes you are new in-role, with or without an existing CoE around you. It is deliberately sequenced: do not try to build everything at once.

### Days 1–30 — observe, meet, draft

**Goal: understand the organization's AI reality before you commit to anything.**

Week 1 — meet your boss, your HR partner, your legal partner, your security partner, your governance counterpart (whoever they are — `head-of-ai-governance` if the role exists; the privacy officer or the CIO if not), and your CISO partner. No plans, no proposals; questions and listening.

Week 2 — meet five BU leaders across the functions AI touches most (engineering, product, operations, customer-facing, back-office). Ask: what tools are you using, what have you tried that didn't work, what would you pay for from a CoE, what are you afraid of, what are you proud of.

Week 3 — meet the people doing the work. Run three or four skip-level conversations with individual contributors in the BUs you've met. Ask the same questions. Look especially for the shadow AI — tools people use that are not on the approved envelope.

Week 4 — synthesize. Write a short landscape memo: who is using what, what is working, what is on fire, where the gaps are. Share it with your boss and your governance counterpart; invite correction.

**Artifacts by end of day 30:**
- Draft scope contract (Chapter 01) — version 0.1, not signed yet.
- Draft CoE shape choice (Chapter 02) — with rationale that names specific organizational facts.
- Landscape memo (above).

### Days 31–60 — align, sign, publish

**Goal: convert the draft into agreement and set up the operating model.**

Week 5 — iterate the scope contract with your boss and governance counterpart. Push back on scope creep; accept the parts you agree with; name what's out. Sign.

Week 6 — iterate the CoE shape with your executive sponsor. If they are a CAIO, this is their meeting; if they are CIO or COO, you brief them. Write the one-paragraph rationale and get a thumbs-up.

Week 7 — draft the operating model (Chapter 03). Intake path, decision rights table, funding model, staffing shape and service-catalog v0.1. Share with governance, security, and the top two BU leaders for redline. Expect pushback on decision rights; welcome it — the pushback is where the real agreement forms.

Week 8 — publish v1.0 of the operating model to your partner audience (not the whole organization yet). Set the first intake triage (weekly). Set the first exec reporting cadence (monthly to your boss, quarterly to whichever body the scope contract names).

**Artifacts by end of day 60:**
- Signed scope contract (v1.0).
- CoE shape decision + rationale (v1.0), ratified by executive sponsor.
- Operating-model document (v1.0), ratified by governance, security, and representative BU leaders.
- Decision-rights table (v1.0).
- Service catalog v0.1.
- Intake path open to a small invite group of champions for traffic.

### Days 61–90 — ship something small, measure, iterate

**Goal: run the operating model on real traffic and prove it works.**

Week 9 — open the intake path to a pilot cohort (one BU, two teams). Run the first weekly triage. Expect messes in the intake form, the questionnaire, the hand-off to governance — note them and iterate.

Week 10 — launch v1 of the Level 1 literacy curriculum to the pilot cohort. It does not have to be a finished product; a two-hour instructor-led session plus a short recorded primer is enough to test the scaffolding. Record completions as the Article 4 evidence trail (Chapter 05) from day 1.

Week 11 — pick the first rollout to run through the full operating model. Something low-stakes but real — an approved-tool access expansion, not the launch of a flagship copilot. The point is to flush the operating model with a real workload.

Week 12 — write the first exec scorecard. Honest numbers; small volumes; frame the quarter as "the operating model is live and measured" — not "we rolled out AI." Hand to your boss; brief the sponsor; publish to governance.

**Artifacts by end of day 90:**
- Live intake with at least one real pilot cohort going through it.
- v1 of the Level 1 literacy curriculum delivered to at least one cohort, with evidence captured.
- First rollout artifact bundle shipped end-to-end.
- First executive scorecard delivered with honest numbers.
- Operating model v1.1 with the corrections the first cohort found.

## Common first-90-days traps

- **Over-committing to a flagship tool rollout in the first 60 days.** You don't know enough yet. Run through the operating model with something small first.
- **Treating the scope contract as optional.** You will regret this in week 20 when someone hands you work that isn't yours and you have no written answer.
- **Hiring the staffing shape all at once.** You have not yet proven the operating model. Hire one role at a time, after you have load to justify it.
- **Writing a service catalog that is longer than the first 90 days can ship.** The catalog is a promise. Keep it small until you can deliver what's on it.

## Summary

- AIGP is the vocabulary of your governance partners; read the body of knowledge even if you don't sit the exam.
- ATD and Prosci are the vocabularies of your L&D and change-management partners; expect those credentials on the leads under you.
- Know NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act, and OECD AI Principles well enough to discuss them without a glossary.
- Run the 90-day plan deliberately: observe and draft (days 1–30); align, sign, publish (days 31–60); ship, measure, iterate (days 61–90).
- By day 90 you have a signed scope contract, a ratified operating model, a running intake, a delivered curriculum cohort, a first rollout through the full pipeline, and an honest exec scorecard. Nothing more; nothing less.
