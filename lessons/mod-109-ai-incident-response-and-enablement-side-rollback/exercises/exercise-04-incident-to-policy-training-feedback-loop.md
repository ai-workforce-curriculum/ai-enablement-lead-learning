# Exercise 04 — Incident-to-Policy-and-Training Feedback Loop

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours
**Format:** post-incident review record + per-surface revision drafts + three-month verification schedule + council-brief page, Markdown
**Grounding chapters:** [06 — The Close-the-Loop Discipline](../06-the-close-the-loop-discipline.md); [07 — Briefing the Governance Council and Supporting Article 73 Reporting](../07-briefing-the-governance-council-and-supporting-article-73-reporting.md). Prior reading: [mod-103 Chapter 01](../../mod-103-policy-co-authoring-with-governance/01-the-co-author-posture-and-the-redline-log.md); [mod-105 Chapter 04](../../mod-105-champion-networks-and-change-management/04-the-rollout-communications-cadence.md); [mod-106](../../mod-106-outcome-measurement-and-executive-reporting/README.md).

## Objective

Take the plausible confirmed incident you have been running through Exercises 01, 02, and 03 and close the loop. Produce the post-incident review record, the per-surface revision drafts across the five surfaces from Chapter 06, the three-month verification schedule, and the enablement-side section of the governance-council brief (four-part read-ahead from Chapter 07). The output is the artefact bundle that makes the organisation's learning auditable and the next rollout better than the one that produced the incident.

## Prerequisites

- Chapters 06 and 07 read.
- [mod-103 Chapter 01](../../mod-103-policy-co-authoring-with-governance/01-the-co-author-posture-and-the-redline-log.md) read.
- [mod-105 Chapter 04](../../mod-105-champion-networks-and-change-management/04-the-rollout-communications-cadence.md) read.
- [mod-106](../../mod-106-outcome-measurement-and-executive-reporting/README.md) read for the exec-scorecard incident-signal surface.
- Exercises 01, 02, and 03 complete. The triage log, runbook, and three handoff contracts are inputs; so is the rollout you have been carrying.

## Problem statement

Pick one of the four signals from Exercise 01 as a plausible **confirmed** incident. The downstream investigation has produced a finding (you simulate the finding plausibly; flag the simulation). The governance side has produced a severity classification and authorised the close-the-loop. Your job is to run the close-the-loop discipline through the five-step cadence from Chapter 06 and to populate the enablement-side section of the council brief from Chapter 07.

The incident is confirmed; the loop must close. You have three hours.

## Requirements

### Part A — The post-incident review record

A one-to-two-page record of the review from Chapter 06 Step 1. Required sections:

1. **Incident summary.** Three to five sentences; the incident shape, source, severity (as classified by the governance side), affected scope.
2. **Timeline-from-the-enablement-side-log.** The compressed timeline from your Exercise 01 triage log, plus the subsequent two-week supporting-mode horizon from your Exercise 03 check-ins.
3. **What worked.** Three to five specific operational-substance observations about the enablement side's first hour and supporting-mode performance. Not platitudes; specific moves and their results.
4. **What did not work.** Three to five specific observations. If one of the three postures from Chapter 01 was stressed, name it.
5. **Five-surface walk.** For each of the five surfaces (mod-102 artifact set, mod-103 policy stack, mod-104 training, mod-105 champions, mod-106 scorecard), name whether the surface revises or not, and the specific revision scope. Not all five will revise.
6. **Revision plan.** For each revising surface: owner (default enablement lead; some co-own), window (short/standard/long), and the specific deliverable.

### Part B — Per-surface revision drafts

Draft the revisions for each surface that revises. Not full re-authoring; the specific revision draft that would enter the walking-redline cycle or the L&D partnership. For each revising surface, produce:

- **mod-102 rollout artifact set revisions** (one or more of: use policy, SOP, access playbook, support ladder, champion list, kill-switch runbook, approved-envelope-position change). Each revision is written as the specific text change to the existing artefact — the before/after lines or the new section. Co-author tags per [mod-103 Chapter 01](../../mod-103-policy-co-authoring-with-governance/01-the-co-author-posture-and-the-redline-log.md).
- **mod-103 policy stack revisions** (AUP, SOP, intake questionnaire). Written as before/after lines against the current policy text. If the incident surfaces an intake-questionnaire schema gap, write the evidence-shaped memo to `senior-ai-governance-architect` per [mod-103 Chapter 06](../../mod-103-policy-co-authoring-with-governance/06-the-intake-questionnaire.md) — not a schema proposal.
- **mod-104 training content revisions** (operator-tier, specialist-tier, use-case library, role-sub-tier). Each revision at outline-level (not full module); the specific topic area, learning outcome, and attestation question if applicable.
- **mod-105 champion-network priorities revisions** — champion recognition (named format), focused briefing for the affected function (one-paragraph brief), champion-training case-library addition (one-paragraph case write-up).
- **mod-106 exec-scorecard incident-signal row** — the specific row the incident adds to the scorecard's incident-signal surface for this reporting period.

Any surface that does **not** revise gets a one-sentence rationale in the record.

### Part C — Three-month verification schedule

A short calendar-schedule document showing, for each revision, the specific verification-check that lands at the three-month mark. Each verification check has:

- Revision being verified.
- Owner of the check.
- What specifically is checked (policy in published state; training module completion rates; runbook currency; champion brief delivered; scorecard row populated).
- The specific action if the check fails.

### Part D — The council-brief enablement-side section

The four-part read-ahead from Chapter 07, authored for your confirmed incident:

1. **Signal and recognition.** With the specific recognition-latency number from your Exercise 01 timeline.
2. **First-hour operations.** With the specific kill-switch state, preservation state, and communications state from your Exercises 01 and 02.
3. **Close-the-loop status.** Which revisions are drafted, which are co-signed, which are live. The three-month verification date.
4. **Enablement-side lessons for the next rollout.** Two or three sentences at the altitude the council reads; the substantive content is the revision drafts in Part B, so the section is interpretive, not re-authoring.

Target length: one page total, with the four sections tight.

### Part E — The Article 73 (or parallel obligation) support note

A one-paragraph note identifying whether the incident would plausibly trigger an Article 73 submission (if the organisation is EU-touching), a GDPR Article 33 breach notification, a HIPAA Breach Notification Rule submission, a state-law notification, or a sector-specific obligation — and what the enablement side's specific supporting work was (or would be) under each applicable regime. Name the regime; name the enablement-side input; name the author of the submission (not the enablement lead). If no obligation applies, say so and name the reasoning.

## Starter guidance

- The hardest part of the drill is deciding which surfaces **do not** revise. Over-revision is as much a failure mode as under-revision; a confirmed operator-specific incident with no generalisable lesson does not revise training and does not revise policy. Chapter 06 names four shapes where the loop does not call for a revision; use them.
- The revision drafts should be **specific text**, not described. "Revise section 3 to tighten the data-class language" is not a revision; the before/after lines are. The value of the artefact bundle is the specificity.
- The exec-scorecard incident-signal row should match the language and format of your mod-106 scorecard (from mod-106 work). If you have not built the scorecard, use a plausible format.
- The three-month verification schedule is the artefact that makes the loop close in practice, not in principle. Treat it as calendar entries with owners.
- The council-brief section is tight. Resist the temptation to expand beyond one page; the governance side's content takes the rest of the council's time per Chapter 07.
- The Article 73 / parallel-obligation note is likely a "not triggered" note for most training-drill scenarios. Say so and name why; the reasoning is the valuable part, not the eventual trigger.
- Where an organisational fact you need is not knowable (the L&D partner's authoring window; the exact exec-scorecard template), mark `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` inline.

## Acceptance criteria

- The post-incident review record has all six required sections.
- Each revising surface produces a specific revision draft with before/after lines (not described revisions).
- Each non-revising surface has a one-sentence rationale in the record.
- The three-month verification schedule has an owner, a specific check, and a fail-action per revision.
- The council-brief section is one page with all four parts.
- The Article 73 / parallel-obligation note names the applicable regimes (even if to say "not triggered") and names the submission author.
- All revisions are tagged to the incident ID in a way that an auditor can trace back.
- `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` used wherever an organisational fact is missing.

## Stretch goals

- Author the **"what-the-next-rollout-does-differently"** memo — one page — distilling the revision drafts into forward operating guidance for the next rollout that uses the same tool category or vendor. This is the artefact that most cleanly expresses the organisational learning.
- Draft the **"redline-log-entry"** for each revision in the form the mod-103 Chapter 01 redline log actually uses, including the incident ID tag. This is the audit-trail primitive that makes the close-the-loop reviewable years later.
- Produce the **"honest-slot-content"** for the next quarterly cadence slot 3 from [mod-105 Chapter 04](../../mod-105-champion-networks-and-change-management/04-the-rollout-communications-cadence.md). Two paragraphs; what the organisation says about the incident and what it has done about it. This is the content your champions carry; write it well.

## Why this exercise matters

An incident that does not revise an artefact three months later was survived, not learned from. The close-the-loop discipline is the procedure that forces the learning to materialise. Producing the artefact bundle under a three-hour budget is calibration for producing it against each confirmed incident, across quarters and years, so that the organisation's learning is the audit-readable chain regulators and governance councils expect. The paired artefact bundle across Exercises 01–04 is the operational portfolio the enablement lead carries into the role — a coherent read of how incident response works on the enablement side, from signal recognition to loop closure.
