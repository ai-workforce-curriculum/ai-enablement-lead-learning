# The Industry-Flavour Problem

> The first seven modules have built the enablement operating model as if the organization were industry-neutral. That was deliberate — the operating model, the policy skeleton, the training curriculum, the champion network, the scorecard, the budget, all work the same shape in a SaaS company, a bank, a hospital system, and a publisher. The *content* of those artifacts does not. This module is where the industry-neutral shape meets the industry-specific flavour, and where the enablement lead learns which parts of the role change on contact with a regulated vertical.

## What "industry flavour" means in this track

A *flavour* is not a different job. It is the industry- and regulator-specific vocabulary, obligations, data classes, counterpart roles, and default postures that sit on top of the industry-neutral shape. The level-45 role is the same role at Navitus Health Solutions, Wells Fargo, Disney, and McGraw Hill — the handoffs from [mod-101 Chapter 04](../mod-101-role-and-the-org-wide-enablement-operating-model/04-handoffs-with-adjacent-roles.md) still describe who owns what, the co-author posture from [mod-103 Chapter 01](../mod-103-policy-co-authoring-with-governance/01-the-co-author-posture-and-the-redline-log.md) still describes how policy is written, the Article 4 floor from [mod-101 Chapter 05](../mod-101-role-and-the-org-wide-enablement-operating-model/05-eu-ai-act-article-4-and-the-literacy-floor.md) still describes the deployer literacy obligation if the organization touches the EU market. What changes is the *content that fills each slot*.

Three concrete examples of flavour, drawn forward from the chapters that follow:

- At a healthcare organization, the data-handling SOP from [mod-103 Chapter 05](../mod-103-policy-co-authoring-with-governance/05-the-data-handling-sop.md) has to name protected health information (PHI) as a data class with its own handling rule, and the vendor-vetting rubric from [mod-107 Chapter 06](../mod-107-budget-roi-and-build-vs-buy-at-org-scope/06-the-vendor-vetting-rubric.md) has to require a signed business associate agreement (BAA) before the tool enters the approved envelope. The *SOP shape* is unchanged; the *content* is HIPAA-shaped.
- At a bank, the intake questionnaire from [mod-103 Chapter 06](../mod-103-policy-co-authoring-with-governance/06-the-intake-questionnaire.md) has to carry a model-risk classification field that routes any model-like candidate to the model risk management (MRM) function under SR 11-7, and the training curriculum from [mod-104](../mod-104-training-programs-and-ai-literacy-curriculum/README.md) has to layer in FINRA supervision and recordkeeping content for roles that produce customer communications. Shape unchanged, content banking-shaped.
- At a legal engineering function inside a media conglomerate, the acceptable-use policy from [mod-103 Chapter 04](../mod-103-policy-co-authoring-with-governance/04-the-one-page-acceptable-use-policy.md) has to carry explicit prohibitions around attorney work product, privilege, and client-confidentiality classes. Shape unchanged, content legal-shaped.

The role is portable across flavours; the artifacts are not. The enablement lead who tries to port a SaaS AUP into a bank without re-authoring it against the bank's regulatory reality will produce a policy that reads as unserious to the counterparts whose signatures it needs.

## The grounding — R19 in the sample

Across the 26 postings read in the live sample behind this track, seven name industry-regulatory specificity as a material slice of the role. Call it R19 — industry-and-regulatory-flavour weight — at 0.27 (7/26). That sits below the primary-responsibility threshold used elsewhere in the track, but it is above the floor where the content is optional. In practice, that 27% breaks down roughly as follows across the four industry families this module covers:

- **Healthcare / pharmacy benefits.** `p-018` Navitus Health Solutions (Lead AI Governance & Enablement, pharmacy benefits manager with direct PHI exposure); `p-008` WelbeHealth (AI Adoption & Enablement Lead, PACE-model care provider).
- **Finance.** `p-014` Wells Fargo (AI Enablement Lending Lead, bank regulated under the Federal Reserve and the OCC); `p-012` Dodge & Cox (AI Enablement Manager, SEC-registered investment adviser); `p-022` Common Securitization Solutions (Director, Transformation & AI Enablement, GSE-adjacent mortgage infrastructure).
- **Legal engineering.** `p-021` The Walt Disney Company (Director, AI Enablement & Legal Engineering, legal function inside a media conglomerate).
- **Education / learning.** `p-009` ed-tech client (Experis USA posting, AI Enablement Lead, ed-tech vendor); `p-013` Ridgeline (AI Enablement Lead, investment-management platform with education-sector-flavoured content); `p-017` McGraw Hill (Director, Engineering — AI Enablement, educational publisher with K-12 and higher-ed exposure).

Below the threshold is a signal, not an exemption. Two readings of R19 at 0.27 matter:

- **Any enablement lead in one of those four industries meets the flavour on day one.** The 27% is the probability *across* the sample that the role is flavour-heavy. For a role *inside* one of these industries, the probability is 100%. The banking enablement lead will co-author the AUP against SR 11-7; the healthcare enablement lead will co-author the SOP against HIPAA; these are not the long tail of the job, they are the shape of the first week.
- **The industry-neutral enablement lead still reads this module.** Industry-neutral postings do not stay industry-neutral forever — organizations acquire regulated lines of business, open an EU subsidiary, buy a competitor with a healthcare product. The flavour arrives mid-tenure. The enablement lead who has read the shape of the four flavours before the acquisition closes is cheaper to stand up than the one who learns the first flavour on contact.

## Why the industry-neutral playbook breaks on contact

Three recurring failure modes when an industry-neutral enablement lead walks into a regulated industry with the industry-neutral artifact set:

- **The data classes in the AUP do not match the regulatory data taxonomy.** A generic AUP names "confidential information" and "personal data." A healthcare AUP must name PHI by its HIPAA definition; a bank AUP must name customer non-public personal information (NPI) under Gramm-Leach-Bliley and material non-public information (MNPI) under securities law; a legal engineering AUP must name attorney work product and privileged communications. If the AUP's data classes do not map to the regulator's data classes, every downstream handling rule is unanchored.
- **The approved-use list does not survive the regulator's reading.** A generic coding-assistant AUP permits "generating code for production features." A bank's model risk team reads the same line and asks whether the tool is producing *decision-support logic* that gets used in a regulated decision (credit, suitability, fair lending). If the answer is yes, the tool is a candidate "model" under SR 11-7, and the approved-use line has committed the organization to running it through MRM before production use. The industry-neutral line could not see the question coming.
- **The scorecard and the measurement surface omit regulatory evidence.** The exec scorecard from [mod-106](../mod-106-outcome-measurement-and-executive-reporting/README.md) is a business-performance surface. In a regulated industry, the scorecard also has to carry — or route to — regulatory-evidence surfaces: Article 4 completion rosters for EU-touching orgs, SR 11-7 model-inventory status for banks, PHI-handling incident counts for healthcare, FERPA-compliance evidence for ed-tech serving K-12 customers. A scorecard that reports adoption and velocity without any regulatory surface is a scorecard that will be re-written the first time the audit or exam committee reads it.

Each failure has the same root: industry-neutral artifacts produced without a flavour-aware co-author on the signing surface. The fix is not to re-author the artifacts alone — the enablement lead does not have the standing or the knowledge to do that — but to *invite the flavour-aware counterparts into the co-authoring cycle from the first draft*. That is the posture this module is building.

## The chapters ahead and how they compose

- **Chapter 02** builds the Article 4 obligation from [mod-101 Chapter 05](../mod-101-role-and-the-org-wide-enablement-operating-model/05-eu-ai-act-article-4-and-the-literacy-floor.md) from a floor into a concrete training-content design — who is trained on what by when, with the content outline at the depth the regulator will read. Article 4 belongs in the industry module because the EU-touching surface is the first flavour that lands on top of the industry-neutral operating model for most global organizations.
- **Chapters 03 through 06** walk the four industry families — healthcare, finance, legal engineering, education — grounded in the specific postings above. Each chapter follows the same shape: the regulators and standards the enablement lead meets, the data classes that attach to the industry, the artifact-level deltas against the industry-neutral baseline, the counterpart roles, and the drift the enablement lead most often gets pulled into.
- **Chapter 07** draws the explicit line between the enablement-side obligations in each industry and the control-ownership obligations owned by `head-of-ai-governance` (level 60) and `senior-ai-governance-architect` (level 50). The split from [mod-101 Chapter 04](../mod-101-role-and-the-org-wide-enablement-operating-model/04-handoffs-with-adjacent-roles.md) survives industry change; the content on either side of the line does not.
- **Chapter 08** is the partnering chapter — how the enablement lead works with industry-specific legal, compliance, and privacy counterparts without taking legal positions and without becoming the de-facto owner of their obligations.

The exercises close the loop. Exercises 01 through 03 are drills against the industry chapters; exercises 04 and 05 are the industry-adjusted versions of the mod-103 policy package and the mod-104 training curriculum, one industry of the learner's choice. Carrying a single industry across all five exercises produces a coherent artifact bundle for that industry that can live next to the industry-neutral baseline from mod-103 and mod-104.

## What this module does not cover

- **Deep regulatory law.** HIPAA, SR 11-7, FINRA, FERPA, COPPA, the EU AI Act, and the case law each has accumulated are each a professional discipline of their own. This module teaches the enablement lead enough vocabulary and structure to partner with the lawyers and compliance officers who hold those disciplines, not to replace them. See Chapter 08 for the posture that stops the drift.
- **Every industry that has a flavour.** The sampled postings behind the track did not surface meaningful traffic from defense, public sector, energy, aerospace, insurance, or heavily-regulated manufacturing. Those flavours exist and would each take a chapter of their own; readers in those industries can read Chapters 03 through 06 for the *method* of flavour-mapping and apply it to their own regulators. <!-- needs-research: if the posting sample refreshes to surface defense or public-sector weight above the flavour threshold, add chapters covering FedRAMP, CMMC, DFARS, and public-sector procurement posture. -->
- **Full industry-specific training curricula.** The industry-adjusted mod-104 curriculum (exercise 05) is one industry, at the level the role would hand to an instructional-design partner. The complete curriculum build is still the mod-104 artifact; the industry version is the delta.

## Summary

- A *flavour* is the industry- and regulator-specific vocabulary, data classes, obligations, counterpart roles, and default postures that sit on top of the industry-neutral operating model. The role is portable; the artifacts are not.
- R19 in the posting sample (0.27, 7/26, below threshold but material) grounds the four industry families this module covers: healthcare, finance, legal engineering, education.
- Industry-neutral artifacts break on contact with a regulated industry in three predictable ways: data-class mismatch, approved-use overreach, and missing regulatory evidence on the scorecard.
- The fix is co-authoring with flavour-aware counterparts from the first draft, not re-authoring alone.
- Chapters 02 through 06 build the flavour-aware content; Chapters 07 and 08 hold the boundary against drift.
