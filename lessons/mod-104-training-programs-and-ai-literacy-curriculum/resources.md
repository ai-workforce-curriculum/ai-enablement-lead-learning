# Resources for mod-104 — Training Programs, AI Literacy Curriculum, and Prompt-Coaching for Non-Engineers

Primary sources first; secondary sources only where they add something a primary source does not cover. Where a link breaks, search for the title on the issuer's site — URLs move more often than titles.

## Regulation and standards

- **EU AI Act — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.** Full consolidated text on EUR-Lex: <https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj>. Article 4 ("AI literacy") is the operative article for this module. In application since 2025-02-02. See [mod-101 Chapter 05](../mod-101-role-and-the-org-wide-enablement-operating-model/05-eu-ai-act-article-4-and-the-literacy-floor.md) for the deeper treatment.
- **European Commission — AI Office.** Primary source: <https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-office>. The EU body coordinating implementation of the AI Act; watch for Article 4-specific guidance as enforcement matures. <!-- needs-research: catalog the AI Office's published Article 4 guidance documents as of 2026-11 and the member-state-level enforcement notes. -->
- **NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)** and the **Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1).** Primary source: <https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework>. The Govern / Map / Measure / Manage functions include "AI actor competence" subcategories that your literacy program defends against in US-anchored organizations.
- **ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Management system.** Standard page: <https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html>. The international AI management-system standard; its clauses on "competence" and "awareness" interoperate directly with Article 4-style literacy obligations.
- **OECD AI Principles.** Primary source: <https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles>. Intergovernmental AI principles, updated 2024; the "building human capacity" principle is the international reference-point for workforce AI literacy.

## Credentials and bodies of knowledge for the training function

- **ATD — Association for Talent Development.** Capability Model: <https://www.td.org/capability-model>. CPTD and APTD credentials reachable from the ATD site. Your L&D partner almost certainly works from this frame; the Partnership Contract (Exercise 07) sits more easily when both parties speak ATD vocabulary.
- **Josh Bersin — AI in HR and L&D research.** Primary source: <https://joshbersin.com/>. The Josh Bersin Company and Bersin Academy publish ongoing research on how enterprises build AI capability in the workforce. Read for *shape* (what mature programs do), not as evidence of your program's performance. <!-- needs-research: refresh the Bersin citations against the most recent AI-in-L&D publications as of 2026. -->
- **IAPP AIGP — AI Governance Professional.** Program page: <https://iapp.org/certify/aigp/>. Even if you do not sit the exam, review the Body of Knowledge — it is the governance partners' vocabulary and shapes how literacy content is reviewed for compliance alignment.
- **Prosci Change Management Methodology and ADKAR model.** Primary source: <https://www.prosci.com/>. The methodology is proprietary but widely referenced; the ADKAR stages (Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement) map onto the delivery-mix sequencing from [Chapter 05](05-the-delivery-mix.md). Most change-management partners you work with will hold Prosci certifications.
- **Kotter 8-Step Process.** Primary source: <https://www.kotterinc.com/methodology/>. Alternative / complementary frame to ADKAR for the broader change work that surrounds the literacy program.

## Workforce AI adoption benchmarks (reference, not evidence)

Use these to orient your organization against the broader workforce; do *not* cite them as evidence that your program is working. [Chapter 07](07-measuring-fluency-growth.md) treats the rule in detail.

- **Microsoft Work Trend Index.** Primary source: <https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index>. The annual Work Trend report and related research publish population-level data on AI usage, operator sentiment, and reported productivity shifts. Useful as *context* for where a workforce sits; not a target. <!-- needs-research: refresh against the most recent Work Trend Index publication as of 2026. -->
- **McKinsey — The State of AI (annual Global Survey).** Primary source: <https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights>. Annual survey of enterprise AI adoption; the training-and-reskilling questions in the survey give useful cross-industry reference points.
- **Deloitte — State of Generative AI in the Enterprise (quarterly).** Primary source: <https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/issues/trust/state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise.html>. The workforce-readiness sections of the quarterly report are the most useful parts for this module.
- **BCG — AI at Work / Build for the Future reports.** Primary source: <https://www.bcg.com/>. Recurring research on enterprise AI adoption and workforce capability-building. Search the site by title; URLs for individual reports change often.

## Incident and risk reference for scenario-building

Modules 4 (data-handling) and 6 (escalation signals) of the foundational curriculum draw their scenarios from real incidents. Both databases are primary-source and keep growing.

- **AI Incident Database (AIID).** Primary source: <https://incidentdatabase.ai/>. Public catalog of AI incidents. Searchable by domain, incident type, and consequence. The best source for anonymized, real-world scenarios for Modules 4 and 6.
- **OECD.AI Incidents Monitor.** Primary source: <https://oecd.ai/en/incidents-methodology>. Intergovernmental tracking of AI incidents and hazards; complements AIID with policy-grade metadata.

## Prompting technique references (for the coaching program, used lightly)

Prompt coaching ([Chapter 06](06-prompt-coaching-for-non-engineers.md)) is coaching, not teaching. These references are source material for building the coach's diagnostic repertoire — they are *not* material you put into an operator-facing curriculum.

- **Anthropic — Prompt engineering overview.** Primary source: <https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/overview>. The vendor's own documentation on prompting; useful shape for the diagnostic repertoire a coach carries.
- **OpenAI — Prompt engineering guide.** Primary source: <https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/prompt-engineering>. Likewise.
- **Google — Prompt design strategies (Gemini API).** Primary source: <https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/prompting-intro>. Useful for the pattern overlap across vendors; your approved-tool envelope will often include multiple vendors.

The three vendor guides agree on more than they disagree on; a coach's diagnostic repertoire is shaped by the overlapping techniques (clear role / task / format specification; worked examples; iterative refinement; verification habits) rather than by vendor-specific flourishes.

## How to use these resources

- The regulation and standards section should be read in the primary source. Paraphrases drift; exact wording matters when governance partners quote it.
- The ATD / Josh Bersin / IAPP / Prosci / Kotter references are *vocabulary*, not completion. Read the bodies of knowledge; sit the exams only if the career plan justifies it (covered in [mod-112](../mod-112-career-positioning-and-the-next-step-on-the-ladder/README.md)).
- The benchmarks section is for orientation — do not cite them as evidence for the program's own performance. The denominators are not comparable to your organization's.
- The incident databases are evidence for Modules 4 and 6 scenario design — they replace speculative "what could go wrong" with recorded "what did go wrong."
- The prompting technique references stay inside the coach's curriculum. Do not push them into operator-facing content; operators do not need to read vendor prompt-engineering guides.
