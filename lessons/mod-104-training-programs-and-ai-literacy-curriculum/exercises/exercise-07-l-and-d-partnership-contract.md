# Exercise 07 — L&D Partnership Contract

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours
**Format:** a signed-style partnership contract of 3–5 pages, Markdown or docx
**Grounding chapters:** [08 — Partnering with HR, L&D, and Internal Comms](../08-partnering-with-hr-ld-and-internal-comms.md), [05 — The Delivery Mix](../05-the-delivery-mix.md)

## Objective

Produce the **L&D partnership contract** — the one artifact that defines the relationship between your enablement program and the L&D organization that delivers most of the LMS-bound curriculum. The contract is the artifact that survives counterpart turnover and that gets the AI literacy program delivered without you teaching every class.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter 08 read; Chapter 05 skimmed.
- Fluency target map from Exercise 01 and foundational curriculum outline from Exercise 02 in hand (they shape what the contract actually commits to).
- A clear picture of your organization's L&D function — size, maturity, LMS platform, whether they run on an annual catalog cycle. If you do not have real data, pick one of:
  - a mature L&D team with a seasoned CPTD-credentialed director and an enterprise LMS
  - a lean L&D function with one director and a basic LMS, mostly outsourcing content design
  - a post-merger L&D function running two overlapping LMSes and a planned migration

## Problem statement

You are two weeks into the role and your L&D counterpart has asked for a working meeting to "set up how we're going to do this together." You will walk in with the contract draft. By the end of the meeting, you want a redlined v0.9 both sides agree is close enough to sign, with the open items captured for the next meeting.

Chapter 08 names the five sections. This exercise is where you author them for a real relationship.

## Requirements

Deliver one contract document with the following sections. Match the Chapter 08 structure.

### 1. Cover material

- Contract title (e.g., "AI Enablement ↔ Learning & Development — Partnership Contract").
- The two signing parties by role (not name): AI Enablement Lead and the L&D counterpart's role.
- Effective date, next review date (annual).
- Version.
- A one-paragraph handover note at the top — three sentences describing the biggest open items as of this version, so a future counterpart can orient in week one.

### 2. Shared goal (3–5 sentences)

A non-platitude statement of what the partnership is for. Example shape (adapt to your organization):

> The partnership delivers the AI literacy foundational curriculum to the in-scope population with Article 4-defensible evidence, on an annual refresh cadence and with trigger-based updates, at a cycle time that keeps pace with the approved-tool envelope.

Specify the in-scope population size and the key regulatory or risk posture driving the work.

### 3. Roles and responsibilities (table, one row per major activity)

A short table with columns for Activity | AI Enablement | L&D | Decider | Evidence owner. At minimum, name rows for:

- Foundational curriculum authoring and SME review.
- Instructional design and LMS build-out.
- LMS admin and completion record-keeping.
- Knowledge-check item authoring.
- Rubric authoring (ties to Exercise 06).
- Role-based playbook curriculum conversion for LMS-bound content.
- Vendor / seat-license purchasing for commercial learning content.
- Refresh execution when a trigger fires.
- Audit response for the Article 4 evidence pack.

### 4. Interfaces and cadences

- Standing partnership meeting (biweekly or monthly) — length, standing agenda.
- The shared backlog — where it lives, who prioritizes.
- Escalation path when the standing meeting is not enough (named roles above each counterpart).
- Fast-lane carve-outs from the Chapter 08 negotiation:
  - Self-serve content (prompt libraries, playbooks, envelope updates): L&D is reviewer, not owner; content lives on a wiki/equivalent.
  - Spotlight content: 48-hour review lane.
  - Formal LMS-bound content: standard cycle.

### 5. Shared metrics (3–5 named metrics)

Both parties are accountable for each metric. At minimum include:

- Completion rate against in-scope roster, by tier.
- Pass rate on knowledge checks.
- Cycle time from content change (policy or envelope change) to LMS-live.
- Lapsed-record rate (completion records older than the refresh cadence).
- Audit-pack retrieval time (how long to assemble the Article 4 evidence pack if an auditor asks).

Each metric has a target and a cadence for reporting.

### 6. Explicit boundaries (what this partnership does NOT cover)

The hardest-to-skip section. Spell out at least five items with the role each one routes to. Starter list:

- Team-scope delivery → `ai-augmented-team-manager`.
- Control-library architecture → `senior-ai-governance-architect`.
- Legal opinions → counsel.
- AI-misconduct enforcement and discipline → HR (co-author with legal).
- Hiring-assessment workflow design → HR (not this partnership).
- IC-engineering depth → `ai-assisted-software-engineer`.
- IC-analyst depth → `ai-augmented-analyst`.

Match the role-naming conventions from the rest of the curriculum.

### 7. Co-authoring and redline hygiene

A short section pointing at [mod-103 Chapter 01](../../mod-103-policy-co-authoring-with-governance/01-the-co-author-posture-and-the-redline-log.md) and specifying:

- Contract changes are made as redlines, not rewrites.
- The redline log for this contract lives at [specified location].
- Annual review process — who kicks it off, when, what review looks like.

## Starter guidance

- The hardest section to write honestly is "explicit boundaries." Your L&D counterpart will want to include items like "team-scope delivery for high-visibility teams." Hold the line; those belong to the team manager with support, not to you or L&D. The contract is where that line gets written once.
- The handover note at the top is unusually important. Imagine this is Y3 of the contract, your L&D counterpart is new in role, and the current version is sitting in the shared drive. What three sentences would orient them? Write those.
- Do not write the contract in abstractions. "L&D owns LMS admin" is useful; "we partner on administration" is not. If a section could fit any partnership, rewrite with specifics.
- The shared metrics are where the contract either earns its keep or fails. If both sides are not held accountable for the same number, the partnership is not shared, it is transactional. Pick three to five numbers and commit.
- Reference Exercise 02 (foundational curriculum) and Exercise 05 (cadence plan) in the Shared Goal section so the contract names the specific work it exists to deliver.

## Deliverables

- `enablement-ld-partnership-contract.md` (or `.docx`): the contract above.
- A short *first-meeting agenda* (one page, bulleted) that you would walk into the working meeting with — the sections you will cover, the sections you expect redlines on, the decisions you want made that day.

## Acceptance criteria

- The contract is 3–5 pages. Shorter and it is under-specified; longer and it is a job description.
- All seven sections from the template are present and non-vacuous.
- Roles-and-responsibilities table has one decider per row.
- Shared metrics are specific numbers with targets and cadences, not categories.
- Explicit boundaries name at least five out-of-scope items with routes.
- The handover note at the top is three sentences and gives an incoming counterpart a working mental model.

## Stretch goals

- Author the corresponding HR partnership contract (shorter, 2–3 pages) using the same template. The HR version will have different boundaries (hiring assessments, discipline) and different metrics (roster accuracy, lapse rate).
- Author the corresponding Internal Comms partnership contract — with the "three messages per month" ceiling encoded.
- Write the "when this contract fails" short note — a paragraph on the recurring ways partnership contracts break (unowned backlog, ignored metrics, counterpart turnover) and the resilience moves this contract has built in.
- Produce the budget / headcount ask that corresponds to this contract — the specific L&D seats and content-licensing budget that back up the metrics. This feeds [mod-107](../../mod-107-budget-roi-and-build-vs-buy-at-org-scope/README.md).

## Why this exercise matters

The partnership contract is the artifact that lets you run a full training program with a CoE team of three or five instead of thirty. If L&D is not held accountable with you for the shared metrics, you will end up owning all the work and getting credit for none of it. Writing one contract correctly produces the template for the next three (HR, Internal Comms, the champion network charter), and the discipline that lets the whole operating model scale.
