# Exercise 03 — Rollout Communications Cadence Plan

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours
**Format:** one artifact of 3–4 pages (table-heavy + sample content), Markdown or docx
**Grounding chapter:** [04 — The Rollout Communications Cadence](../04-the-rollout-communications-cadence.md)

## Objective

Produce the quarter's rollout communications cadence plan for a specific named rollout in a specific organization. The plan shows every send across every surface across the quarter, the four-slot content allocation per send, the audience segmentation, and sample text for the three highest-stakes sends (launch comms, month-two spotlight, end-of-quarter scorecard). The exercise is the surface where [Chapter 04](../04-the-rollout-communications-cadence.md) becomes an operating artifact that could be handed to internal comms and executed from.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter 04 read.
- A named rollout. If you don't have a real one, use: your organization is standing up an **AI-assisted contract-redline tool** for the legal operations function this coming quarter (Q+1). The rollout is going to 40 contract specialists and 8 attorneys in legal ops. The tool has been in pilot with 6 specialists for the last two months.
- The approved-tool envelope (so Slot 2 can reference it) and the acceptable-use policy stub from [mod-103 Exercise 01](../../mod-103-policy-co-authoring-with-governance/exercises/exercise-01-one-page-acceptable-use-policy-drill.md) (so launch comms can point to it).
- Exercise 01's champion network design (so launch comms can name the function's champion).

## Problem statement

Your exec sponsor and the head of legal operations are asking for the cadence plan before the GA date is set. Internal comms is asking for calendar slots. Legal ops is asking what their operators will see and when. The plan has to work for all three audiences at once. It also has to be honest — the cadence must include the dissent slot ([Chapter 04](../04-the-rollout-communications-cadence.md)) at least once.

## Requirements

Deliver one artifact with the following components.

### 1. Rollout context (3–5 sentences)

Short framing: what the rollout is, who the in-scope population is, the opt-in-vs-mandate shape (from [Chapter 05](../05-opt-in-vs-mandate.md); consider this a hint — contract-redline work in a legal ops function has a specific decision shape), the GA date, the quarter the plan covers.

### 2. The quarterly cadence table (the primary artifact)

| Week | Surface | Audience | Slot 1 content | Slot 2 content | Slot 3 content | Slot 4 content | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

One row per send across the quarter. Expect 10–16 rows for a quarter — a mix of monthly org-wide, quarterly function brief, launch comms, spotlights, policy change notices, standing champion-network calls.

Surfaces to populate at minimum:

- Pre-GA week 1 monthly org-wide update (Slot 1 announces the rollout shape).
- Pre-GA week 2 function-targeted communication to legal ops.
- GA week launch comms to legal ops (deep Slot 1→2 transition).
- GA week + 2 champion-network call (field perspective).
- GA week + 4 first spotlight (early use case from a specialist).
- End-of-month 1 monthly org-wide update (Slot 3 names the launch).
- End-of-month 2 spotlight on a second named use case.
- Mid-quarter policy change notice (model: a small SOP clarification driven by field reports).
- End-of-quarter function brief for legal ops.
- End-of-quarter executive scorecard submission (slice of Slot 3 + the ask).
- Continuous self-serve layer updates.

### 3. The three sample sends (sample content)

Draft the actual content for three sends. Each should be at the length and shape the audience would actually see.

#### Sample 3a — Launch comms to legal ops (GA week)

~300 words. All four slots present. Slot 4 names three specific actions (register for the Thursday workshop, review the one-page AUP, join the office hour on Friday). Names the function's champion.

#### Sample 3b — Month-two spotlight

~200 words. A named operator, their specific use case, the before-and-after, the verification habit they built in. This is the content that both lands in the cadence and feeds the use-case library. Written with operator-visible consent.

#### Sample 3c — End-of-quarter executive scorecard slice

~400 words. Slot 3 heavy. Named use cases with measurement; the failed-attempt paragraph (model: a workflow the rollout was expected to help with but did not — be specific); the ask (model: a decision on expanding to a second function in Q+2).

### 4. Audience-segmentation note (paragraph)

One short paragraph on how the cadence differs between the legal ops in-scope audience and the broader organization. Why the function brief exists separately; what is in it that is not in the monthly update; why the organization-wide communication is lighter on detail.

### 5. Partnership-with-internal-comms note (paragraph)

A short paragraph naming the specific handoffs between you and internal comms for this quarter. Who authors what, who publishes where, what the review cycle is, what the escalation path is if an urgent change is needed between scheduled sends.

### 6. Dissent-slot commitment

A single bullet naming the specific send in this quarter's plan where the dissent slot appears — which send, which failed-attempt-or-reversal content, who approves the content ahead of send.

## Starter guidance

- The hardest part of this exercise is Slot 3 in sample 3c. Write it with specificity — "the second-chair attorney on the Q2 merger review used the tool to draft redlines on a non-disclosure amendment; the tool's first pass caught three clauses that would have needed re-redline; the attorney's verification step added two human-only edits; cycle time dropped from 45 to 18 minutes." Not "productivity improved."
- Slot 1 has a credibility cost: things announced here must ship. Err on the conservative side — Q+1 trajectory content should be high-confidence, not aspirational.
- The failed-attempt paragraph in sample 3c is the credibility-building element of the whole document. Write it like you mean it — a small, real thing that did not work, with what you learned and what you changed in response.
- The cadence should not have more than 4 sends per month across all surfaces for the same audience. If it does, you are over-scheduling; the audience will unsubscribe in attention.
- The legal-ops function is a small audience (40–50 operators). A function brief at this scale can be personal — named greetings, named outcomes, named champions. Use the scale.

## Deliverables

- `cadence-plan.md`: the six components above.
- A short **assumption note** (3–5 sentences) at the bottom listing the assumptions you made about the rollout to keep the plan concrete — the tool name, the pilot results, the measurement baselines, the champion name — since the exercise does not fully specify these.

## Acceptance criteria

- The quarterly cadence table has at least 10 rows across at least 5 distinct surfaces.
- Every row has all four slots filled — no "N/A" for Slot 4 (an action is always present, even if small).
- All three sample sends are drafted at the length specified and read as the audience would see them.
- Sample 3b names an operator with consent discipline evident in the content.
- Sample 3c includes the failed-attempt paragraph with specific content.
- The dissent-slot commitment names a specific send, not a general intent.
- The partnership-with-internal-comms note is concrete enough that an internal comms colleague could execute from it.

## Stretch goals

- Author a **second quarter** cadence plan showing how Slot 1 and Slot 3 content shift as the rollout matures — the trajectory content gets closer to delivery, the use-case content grows more concrete, the dissent-slot content changes shape.
- Author a **crisis-mode cadence addendum** — a one-page plan for how the cadence adjusts if an incident surfaces in the function mid-quarter (hand-off from [Chapter 08](../08-the-handoff-to-the-governance-incident-path.md)). What is paused; what is sent; who signs off; how soon the standing cadence resumes.
- Measure the plan against the three cadence failure shapes from [Chapter 04](../04-the-rollout-communications-cadence.md): is any surface at risk of becoming marketing-newsletter, pilot-announcement, or silent? Add a one-paragraph self-critique and the specific adjustment.

## Why this exercise matters

The communications cadence is the surface on which almost every other operating artifact in the program becomes visible. The scorecard narrative, the champion-network attribution, the opt-in-vs-mandate signalling, the resistance response — all of them land (or fail to land) through the cadence. Writing a real quarter's plan with sample content is the practice move that separates an enablement lead who *knows about* cadences from one who can run one credibly.
