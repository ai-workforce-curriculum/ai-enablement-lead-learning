# Exercise 01 — Champion Network Design and Recruit Plan

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours
**Format:** one artifact of 3–5 pages (table-heavy), Markdown or docx
**Grounding chapters:** [01 — The Champion Network as a Change-Management Asset](../01-the-champion-network-as-a-change-management-asset.md), [02 — The Four-Phase Lifecycle](../02-the-four-phase-lifecycle-recruit-train-recognise-retire.md)

## Objective

Produce the two-document pair that the enablement lead presents to the exec sponsor before standing up (or re-standing-up) the champion network: a **standing-network design** describing the asset at steady state, and a **next-cycle recruit plan** describing the specific recruitment the CoE will run in the coming quarter. The pair is what gets you budget, manager-release commitments, and the mandate to open seats.

## Prerequisites

- Chapters 01 and 02 read.
- A specific organization. If you don't have a real one, carry forward one of the three organizations used in [mod-101 Exercise 01](../../mod-101-role-and-the-org-wide-enablement-operating-model/exercises/exercise-01-the-one-page-role-scope-contract.md) so artifacts across modules stack:
  - 3,000-person US bank with existing head of AI governance and no CoE yet
  - 12,000-person global pharma with active CAIO and mature governance
  - 500-person SaaS company with no governance function and a CAIO-who-is-really-the-CTO
- The approved-tool envelope, acceptable-use policy, and training curriculum for the organization — real if you have them, or the artifacts from prior exercises in mod-101, mod-102, mod-103, and mod-104.

## Problem statement

Your exec sponsor is asking for two things, together, in the next steering review: (a) what the champion network will *look like* at steady state — roster size, function coverage, lifecycle cadence, recognition investment — and (b) what the next-quarter recruitment will do specifically, with named functions, open seats, sourcing channel, and a budget ask that can be approved in the same meeting.

They have told you they are not signing off on either document without the other. The standing design without the recruit plan is theoretical; the recruit plan without the standing design is reactive.

## Requirements

Deliver one combined artifact in two parts.

### Part A — Standing network design (2–3 pages)

The asset at steady state, independent of any one rollout.

#### A1. Network scope (3–5 sentences)

Which functions and business units are in scope, which are out, the approximate in-scope operator population, and the roster size you are sizing toward. Use the 1 champion per 50–150 operators rule as a starting shape and adjust for function complexity and regulated-workflow density.

#### A2. Function-coverage table

| Function or BU | In-scope operator count | Target active champions | Current actives (if any) | Priority rank |
|---|---|---|---|---|

One row per function or business unit. Priority rank orders your recruitment pipeline.

#### A3. Lifecycle commitments

A short section (4–6 bullets) naming the organization's commitments for each phase of the lifecycle:

- Recruit: cadence, sourcing-channel priority, selection criteria.
- Train: bootcamp cadence, pairing policy, 30-60-90 discipline.
- Recognise: the three channels operationalised for this organization — visibility into the org, into the career, into the program's direction — and what each looks like concretely (comms, performance-review integration, attribution surface).
- Retire: default term length, retirement artifact, alumni surface.

#### A4. Role-definition table — champion vs. not-champion

Two columns side-by-side:

| What a champion IS | What a champion is NOT |
|---|---|
| 4–6 rows, named for your org | 3–4 rows naming confusions this org is most at risk of (trainer / evangelist / vendor advocate) |

The second column is the one that defends the role from drift in your organization specifically.

#### A5. Steady-state operating calendar

Adapt the calendar table from [Chapter 02](../02-the-four-phase-lifecycle-recruit-train-recognise-retire.md) to this organization — real days, real cadences, real named owners.

### Part B — Next-cycle recruit plan (1–2 pages)

The specific recruitment the CoE runs this quarter, in enough detail that the exec sponsor's sign-off is a yes-or-no decision.

#### B1. Open seats this cycle

| Function | Seats open | Reason (retirement / coverage gap / new rollout) | Target close date |
|---|---|---|---|

Minimum three rows for the first-ever recruit cycle; typically five to eight rows in ongoing cycles.

#### B2. Sourcing plan per seat

Which sourcing channel from [Chapter 02](../02-the-four-phase-lifecycle-recruit-train-recognise-retire.md) you are using per seat (manager nomination / existing-champion referral / open application with manager co-signature / HR-data shortlist), and why.

#### B3. Selection pipeline

The steps from candidate list to selected champion, with named owners at each step. Include:

- Application artifact (what the candidate submits — the 2-paragraph written sample that assesses communication capacity).
- Manager-release confirmation (how the written commitment is captured).
- Selection panel (who, how many, what criteria weighting).

#### B4. Budget ask

A short, specific ask:

- Bootcamp delivery cost for the incoming cohort (facilitator time, materials, operator time at a loaded hourly rate).
- Recognition budget for the next year (modest).
- Ops analyst time to run the lifecycle.
- Any champion-specific tooling (collaboration space, badge in the collab tool, a dedicated office-hours scheduling tool).

The ask is defensible against the headcount coverage from Part A.

#### B5. Risks and mitigations (3–4 bullets)

What could go wrong this cycle and how you are planning to catch it early. Candidate risks: a function with no good manager-nomination candidate; a specific manager unwilling to release time; a selection panel scheduling conflict that pushes the cohort date.

## Starter guidance

- The hardest column in A2 is "target active champions." Fight the temptation to set targets equal to current actives. The target is what the function needs *for field-signal coverage*, not what the function currently has.
- A4 is the chapter's role-definition applied to your org. The "is NOT" column reads as the role's boundary against the drifts most relevant to your context. A 500-person SaaS company's drift risk is different from a 12,000-person pharma's.
- The recruit plan (Part B) is where most of the real decisions live. If B1 and B2 feel abstract, you have not done the organizational work yet — walk the function-coverage table with a specific function leader before drafting.
- Budget-ask realism matters. A number low enough to look painless will not survive the first quarter; a number high enough to invite scrutiny needs a comparison point (other change-management programs in the org, L&D cohort cost, vendor-training cost).
- If the organization has never had a champion network before, Part B's "existing champion referral" channel is empty; sourcing priority shifts to manager nomination.

## Deliverables

- `champion-network-design.md`: Parts A and B above, combined.
- A one-paragraph **exception note** at the bottom (3 sentences max) describing any deliberate deviation from the Chapter 02 defaults — e.g., a two-year default term (rather than one) because your organization's function-level turnover is low, or a three-tier recognition stack that reflects an existing employee-recognition program.

## Acceptance criteria

- Parts A and B both present; the pair holds together — Part B's open seats make sense against Part A's coverage table.
- A2 and B1 have specific function names, not generic placeholders.
- A3's recognition section names three concrete channels, not three generic categories.
- A5's calendar has real days or cadences, not "weekly / monthly / quarterly" labels without specifics.
- B4's budget ask is defensible with a comparison point in the same document.
- The exception note names at least one deliberate deviation from Chapter 02 defaults.

## Stretch goals

- Author a **champion role description** (half a page, suitable for an internal job-posting surface) that a manager could show to a candidate before nominating them. This is the document that converts "we need a champion" into a specific, scoped ask on an operator's time.
- Author the **manager-release form** (half a page) — the artifact a manager actually signs to confirm the 10–20% time commitment. It should name: the operator, the function, the term, the time commitment per week, the reporting line during the term, the performance-review integration.
- Run a tabletop exercise on the selection pipeline with a peer: give them the candidate shortlist for one open seat and run the panel decision. Debrief where your criteria weighting held and where it did not.

## Why this exercise matters

Every downstream artifact in this module and across mod-106, mod-109, mod-110, and mod-111 references the champion network as an operating asset. The Exercise 02 feedback-loop drill assumes this network exists; the Exercise 03 cadence assumes the champions are the carriers; the Exercise 06 dashboard measures this network's retention as a primary signal. Writing the design and the recruit plan first, concretely, saves a half-dozen downstream clarification conversations.
