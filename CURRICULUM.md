## Overview

This track teaches the *org-wide operating system* for AI enablement at level 45: taking ownership of an enterprise AI-adoption agenda across functions and business units — grounded in 26 in-window live postings sampled 2026-11-05 (see [`JOB_REQUIREMENTS.md`](JOB_REQUIREMENTS.md)) — → selecting and rolling out AI tools at scale (three-stage selection, bounded pilots with pre-written decision criteria, the rollout artifact set a tool needs before anyone opens it, outcome / workflow / usage measurement — R1 + R2 + R3 + R4, four of the top six themes in the sample) → co-authoring policy with legal, security, privacy, and governance partners (acceptable-use, data-handling SOP, intake questionnaire — R5 + R13) → designing the audience-segmented AI literacy curriculum and the role-based playbooks that move the organization from aware to fluent (R7 + R10 + R11 + R12 — four requirements grounded in training-program design at 0.85, the single most-cited theme in the sample) → running champion networks as two-way feedback loops and the change-management cadence that keeps the executive scorecard honest (R8 + R9) → owning outcome measurement and executive reporting (adoption / productivity / ROI metrics; weekly scorecard; board-ready quarterly reviews; metric misses published with the reason, not just the hits — R4 depth + R6) → building the budget, ROI, and build-vs-buy story at organization scope (R14) → navigating industry and regulatory flavours of enablement (EU AI Act Article 4 literacy obligation for deployers, healthcare / finance / legal / education flavours — R19) → operating the enablement-side AI incident path with kill switches and rollback playbooks → commissioning (not building) internal AI tools and custom agents from applied-AI and agentic-engineering peers (R16) → running the AI Center of Excellence at steady state — service catalog, intake cadence, decision log, vendor management, cross-functional rituals, champions community-of-practice at org scope (R15 as prereq, R17 as monitored) → and positioning the role for the next step on the ladder (upward to `head-of-ai-governance` / `senior-ai-governance-architect` / `chief-ai-officer` / `ai-engineering-manager`).

It is an **org-wide enablement track at level 45**, positioned in the **AI Workforce family**. The differentiator against [`ai-augmented-team-manager-learning`](https://github.com/ai-workforce-curriculum/ai-augmented-team-manager-learning) (level 40, single-team scope) is *organization scope*: this curriculum builds the operating system across functions, BUs, and vendor relationships — not for one 5-30 person team. The differentiator against [`head-of-ai-governance-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/head-of-ai-governance-learning) (level 60, AI Governance family) is *enablement framing*: this role co-authors policy and reports evidence into the governance program, but does not own the program itself. The differentiator against [`senior-ai-governance-architect-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/senior-ai-governance-architect-learning) (level 50) is *operating vs. architecting*: this role runs the enablement operating model; the architect designs the control library the enablement program plugs into. The differentiator against [`chief-ai-officer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/chief-ai-officer-learning) (level 70) is *executional vs. executive*: this role delivers the operating system; the CAIO sets enterprise AI strategy on top of it.

Total planned commitment: **180 hours across 12 modules** + **160 hours across 3 projects** = **~340 hours**.

## Ownership rule

Following the project-wide ownership rule, this curriculum:

- **Owns** the org-wide AI enablement operating system end-to-end: enablement operating model and CoE pattern choice (hub, hub-and-spoke, federated, embedded); org-wide AI tool selection and rollout (three-stage decision, pilot design, rollout-artifact set, outcome / workflow / usage measurement); policy co-authoring with legal / security / privacy / governance (acceptable-use, data-handling SOP, intake questionnaire); training program and AI literacy curriculum design (audience segmentation, role-based playbooks, fluency measurement); prompt-engineering coaching for non-engineers at the coaching framing; champion-network design and change management across functions; outcome measurement and executive reporting (weekly scorecard, quarterly board-ready review, metric-miss narrative); budget / ROI / build-vs-buy at organization scope; enablement-side industry regulatory flavours (EU AI Act Article 4, HIPAA / FINRA / legal / education); enablement-side incident response and rollback; internal-AI-tool commissioning (as commissioner, not builder); AI CoE steady-state operations (service catalog, intake cadence, decision log, vendor management, cross-functional rituals, org-wide champions community of practice); and the exec-communication story that ties it all together.
- **Defers down** to [`ai-augmented-team-manager-learning`](https://github.com/ai-workforce-curriculum/ai-augmented-team-manager-learning) (level 40) for team-scope AI adoption roadmaps and team-level enablement; to [`ai-augmented-product-manager-learning`](https://github.com/ai-workforce-curriculum/ai-augmented-product-manager-learning) (level 30, AI Workforce family) for AI-feature product scope and ROI framing — a different scope from org-wide enablement ROI; to [`ai-augmented-analyst-learning`](https://github.com/ai-workforce-curriculum/ai-augmented-analyst-learning) (level 20) for IC-level dashboard construction and pipeline building; to [`ai-assisted-software-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/ai-assisted-software-engineer-learning) (level 20) for IC-level prompt craft and agent-authoring mechanics; to [`ai-governance-analyst-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-governance-analyst-learning) (level 15) for the operational governance legwork this role consumes; to a general program-management and people-management background listed in [`PREREQUISITES.md`](PREREQUISITES.md).
- **Defers sideways** to the AI Governance family — [`ai-risk-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-risk-engineer-learning) (peer, level 25) for engineered risk / red-team / guardrail depth this role calls for evidence; [`senior-ai-governance-architect-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/senior-ai-governance-architect-learning) (peer / next-up, level 50) for control-library architecture; [`head-of-ai-governance-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/head-of-ai-governance-learning) (peer / next-up, level 60) for governance program leadership. **Defers sideways** to the AI Engineering family — [`applied-ai-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/applied-ai-engineer-learning) (peer, level 30), [`agentic-ai-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/agentic-ai-engineer-learning), [`agentic-systems-architect-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/agentic-systems-architect-learning) — for the build of internal agents and platforms this role commissions.
- **Defers up** to [`chief-ai-officer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/chief-ai-officer-learning) (level 70) for M&A, portfolio-wide transformation, and executive strategy; to [`ai-engineering-manager-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/ai-engineering-manager-learning) (level 50) for engineering-specific manager-of-managers scope.
- **Out of scope** to legal counsel (this role does not deliver legal opinion), to procurement and finance (contract execution and billing mechanics), to IT identity teams (SSO / IAM implementation), and to security operations / DFIR (SOC-side investigation; this role feeds AI-specific signal to SecOps).

See [`JOB_REQUIREMENTS.md`](JOB_REQUIREMENTS.md) for the requirements-to-coverage map with 26-posting live evidence from the 2026-11 research cycle.

## Module summaries

### mod-101 — The AI Enablement Lead: Role, Scope, and the Org-Wide Enablement Operating Model
Position the level-45 AI Enablement Lead role against `ai-augmented-team-manager` (level 40, single-team), `head-of-ai-governance` (level 60, program), `senior-ai-governance-architect` (level 50, architecture), and `chief-ai-officer` (level 70, executive). Read the AI Center of Excellence pattern family (hub, hub-and-spoke, federated, embedded) and choose one for a concrete organization. Author the enablement operating model — intake path, decision rights, funding model, staffing shape, service catalog. Diagram handoffs with `ai-governance-analyst` (level 15), `ai-risk-engineer` (level 25, peer), and the levels above. Read the EU AI Act Article 4 AI-literacy obligation for deployers as the regulatory floor. Position IAPP AIGP / ATD / Prosci as the credentials vocabulary; build a 90-day entry plan.

### mod-102 — Selecting and Rolling Out AI Tools at Organization Scale
Run the three-stage tool-selection decision at enterprise scope — problem naming, candidate evaluation (build vs. buy, POCs, approved-tooling envelope), and pilot-vs-rollout commit — grounded in R1 (0.42) and R2 (0.31). Design bounded pilots with cohort, duration, baseline, and pre-written adopt / extend / kill decision criteria. Produce the short artifact set a rollout needs before anyone opens the tool — use policy, data-handling SOP, access and offboarding playbook, training package, support and escalation path, champion list, kill-switch and rollback plan — grounded in R3 (0.65). Measure whether the rollout changed anything real — outcome metrics, workflow interviews, usage as sanity-check — grounded in R4 (0.77). Author the pre-mortem that preempts the ghost-user rollout, the metric-theatre pilot, the champion-of-one, and the policy-after-rollout retrofit. Partner with `applied-ai-engineer` (peer, level 30) on commissioned agents and with `ai-risk-engineer` (peer, level 25) on red-team evidence.

### mod-103 — Policy Co-Authoring with Governance, Legal, Security, and Privacy
Co-author acceptable-use, data-handling, and intake policies for AI tools with legal / risk / compliance / infosec — producing documents that read as agreement, not hand-off — grounded in R5 (0.42). Attend the AI governance council as the enablement-side liaison — feed intake workflows, provide risk-classification input, surface field signal back into policy — grounded in R13 (0.54; co-owned with head-of-ai-governance). Read NIST AI RMF 1.0 GOVERN function and ISO/IEC 42001 clauses an enablement lead needs to speak fluently with governance partners, while leaving control-library architecture to `senior-ai-governance-architect` (level 50) and program leadership to `head-of-ai-governance` (level 60). Draft the three policy artifacts the enablement side authors first then redlines with governance. Route legal opinion out to counsel.

### mod-104 — Training Programs, AI Literacy Curriculum, and Prompt-Coaching for Non-Engineers
Design a foundational-to-advanced AI literacy curriculum segmented by audience and skill level (leadership, operators, specialists, builders) with measurable fluency targets — R7 (0.85, the single most-cited theme in the sample) and R10 (0.31). Build role-based playbooks and use-case libraries per function — sales, service, operations, finance, HR, legal, engineering, leadership — grounded in R12 (0.38). Design and run workshops, office hours, lunch-and-learns, bootcamps, adoption challenges, use-case spotlights. Run prompt-engineering coaching for non-engineers at the *coaching* framing — R11 (0.35) — with the explicit boundary that IC-level prompt craft defers down to `ai-assisted-software-engineer` (level 20) and `ai-augmented-analyst` (level 20). Instrument fluency growth with pre / post assessments, task-level proficiency rubrics, and Josh Bersin AI-in-L&D-research-based outcome metrics. Partner with HR / L&D on delivery; hand down team-scope delivery to `ai-augmented-team-manager` (level 40).

### mod-105 — Champion Networks and Change Management for AI Adoption
Design the champion network as a two-way feedback loop — recruit, train, recognise, retire — grounded in R8 (0.54); route champion field signal back into policy (mod-103), tooling choices (mod-102), and training content (mod-104). Run the rollout communications cadence that keeps the executive scorecard honest — grounded in R9 (0.69). Make the opt-in vs. mandate decision deliberately. Handle resistance patterns unique to AI adoption (AI anxiety, role-threat narratives, 'I already have ChatGPT at home', quality skepticism) using Prosci ADKAR and Kotter 8-step as frames adapted for AI-specific resistance. Measure change-management health — adoption curve shape, champion retention, communications reach, resistance survey — and act on each signal with a specific intervention.

### mod-106 — Outcome Measurement and Executive Reporting
Choose adoption / productivity / ROI metrics that distinguish AI-induced gain from AI theatre at org scope — extending R4 (0.77) measurement depth from mod-102 into the org-wide scorecard. Author the executive scorecard — weekly rollup, monthly deep-dive, quarterly board-ready review — that frames AI progress, risks, investment asks, and metric misses; publish the targets that missed with the reason, not just the hits — grounded in R6 (0.58). Produce board updates, LT briefings, strategic memos that a VP can sign. Read the empirical literature on AI productivity (Peng arXiv:2302.06590, Ziegler ACM, Noy & Zhang Science 2023, Brynjolfsson / Li / Raymond NBER, Dell'Acqua et al.) as evidence for metric choice. Partner with Finance on cost-model attribution. Coordinate with `ai-augmented-analyst` (level 20) on dashboard construction and with `ai-augmented-team-manager` (level 40) on team-level measurement this role aggregates.

### mod-107 — Budget, ROI, and Build-vs-Buy at Organization Scope
Build the cost model for enablement — tool licenses, enablement-program spend, embedded enablement partner FTE, training production, champion-network operating cost, support — grounded in R14 (0.35). Project ROI on priority use cases with a defensible value-realisation framework carrying a confidence band. Run the build-vs-buy matrix at enterprise scope (Cursor, Copilot, Claude Code, Windsurf; Microsoft Copilot, Google Gemini for Workspace, Zoom AI Companion; industry-specific tools; internal build). Partner with procurement on contracts (DPA, SOC 2, model-provenance, data-residency terms), with finance on budgeting and cost recovery, with IT on licensing — handing contract execution out of scope. Reconcile the tool stack across BUs so the program does not fund five overlapping copilots. Monitor R17 (vendor / external-partner management, 0.23) with a lightweight vendor-vetting rubric.

### mod-108 — Industry and Regulatory Flavours of Enablement
Translate the EU AI Act Article 4 AI-literacy obligation for deployers into concrete training-program content for EU-touching organizations. Read the industry-specific flavours the enablement lead meets in the sampled postings — healthcare (HIPAA; p-018 Navitus, p-008 WelbeHealth), finance (SR 11-7 enablement slice; p-014 Wells Fargo, p-012 Dodge & Cox, p-022 Common Securitization Solutions), legal engineering (p-021 Disney legal AI), education (p-009 ed-tech, p-013 Ridgeline, p-017 McGraw Hill) — grounded in R19 (0.27, below-threshold but material in-industry). Position enablement-side obligations under each regulator separate from control-ownership obligations owned by `head-of-ai-governance` (level 60) and `senior-ai-governance-architect` (level 50). Partner with industry-specific legal and compliance counterparts without taking legal positions.

### mod-109 — AI Incident Response and Enablement-Side Rollback
Design the enablement-side incident path — what the enablement lead handles when a rollout surfaces harm, where the signal came from (champion field report, usage anomaly, outcome regression, external complaint), the kill-switch, communication to users, evidence preservation, hand-off to governance / SecOps. Operate the rollback plan authored in mod-102, grounding the playbook in AI Incident Database and OECD.AI incidents as precedents. Hand off to `ai-risk-engineer` (peer, level 25) on engineered root-cause, to `head-of-ai-governance` (level 60) on program-level escalation, to security operations / DFIR on SOC-side investigation. Fold confirmed incidents back into mod-102 rollout artifact set, mod-103 policy, mod-104 training content, mod-105 champion priorities. For EU AI Act high-risk systems, support Article 73 serious-incident reporting without authoring it.

### mod-110 — Commissioning Internal AI Tools and Custom Agents
Act as commissioner — not builder — of internal AI tools and custom agents; grounded in R16 (0.38; co-owned with applied-ai-engineer). Author the commissioning brief — problem, audience, success criterion, non-negotiable guardrails, budget, timeline, maintenance commitment — that an `applied-ai-engineer` (peer, level 30) or `agentic-ai-engineer` (peer, AI Engineering family) can build against. Inherit prototypes from billable consultants or business users (R16 evidence: p-019 ClearView Healthcare Partners) — productionize-worthiness assessment, documentation requirement, support commitment, and the honest no-go call. Hand architectural depth to `agentic-systems-architect` (peer, AI Engineering family). Measure commissioned tools on the mod-102 / mod-106 outcome framework — not on a separate internal-build scorecard.

### mod-111 — AI CoE Operations, Vendor Management, and Cross-Functional Leadership
Operate the AI CoE at steady state — intake cadence, service catalog, decision log, status reporting, risk register — applying general program-management practice (R15 prereq) to AI-CoE specifics. Run vendor and external-partner management — screen, train, certify, manage external AI experts, vendors, agencies — grounded in R17 (0.23, below-threshold but monitored). Author the quarterly cross-functional operating ritual — AI council, executive leadership review, BU leadership reviews, functional partnerships (HR, L&D, IT, legal, security, procurement, finance). Lead the AI champions community-of-practice at org scope — the layer above individual team champion networks owned by `ai-augmented-team-manager` (level 40). Position the credentials portfolio (IAPP AIGP, ATD, Prosci, ISO/IEC 42001 lead auditor / implementer, PMP) as shared vocabulary.

### mod-112 — Career Positioning and the Next Step on the Ladder
Author the exec-communication story that ties mod-102 through mod-111 into one coherent narrative a CAIO or board will act on. Choose the next step on the ladder — upward to `head-of-ai-governance` (level 60), to `senior-ai-governance-architect` (level 50), to `chief-ai-officer` (level 70), or sideways to `ai-engineering-manager` (level 50) — and build the deliberate development plan toward it. Position the credentials portfolio externally — AIGP for governance signal, ATD / Prosci for change-management signal, ISO/IEC 42001 for standards signal — selecting the subset that matches the career path. Build the public artifact set (one talk, one open piece of writing, one open-source enablement artifact).

## Assessment

Each module ships **1 quiz** plus the exercises and a lab listed in [`.aicg/curriculum-plan.json`](.aicg/curriculum-plan.json). Each project ships a portfolio-grade README, a reproducibility bundle (policy artifacts, pilot charters, rollout artifact bundles, training-curriculum outlines, champion-network charters, change-management playbooks, scorecard templates, budget and ROI narratives, commissioning briefs, CoE operating manuals, exec-communication narratives), and an explicit rubric covering operating-model coherence, selection and rollout defensibility, policy-package quality, curriculum and champion-network design, measurement honesty, exec-communication quality, budget / ROI defensibility, industry adjustment where applicable, incident-response rigour, commissioning-brief clarity, CoE operations completeness, and cross-functional leadership communication.
